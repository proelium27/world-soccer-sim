# Loans, scouting fog, career charts, retirement, extremism

> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-25 to keep session context small. This is the detailed ledger for this area: read it before touching the code it describes, and add new entries here, not to CLAUDE.md. References to "this file" below meant CLAUDE.md as a whole; the other sections now live alongside this one in `docs/ledger/`.

**Loans** (`core/loans.ts`)
- Fixed 1-3 season move to another roster; flat non-negotiable fee (`computeLoanFee`) + wage handoff (wages key off roster membership, so moving the pid *is* the mechanism). User lists via `/loans` (Accept/Reject only). AI↔AI loans (`runAILoanMarket`) restricted to age ≤ `LOAN_AI_MAX_AGE` (23) and **players outside their club's starting XI** (only buried youngsters). `processLoanReturns` runs early in `simOffseason`. State: `LeagueStore.activeLoans`/`loanListings`/`loanRejections`. No early recall (a disclosed scope cut); the loan-in browsing UI was the other one and shipped 2026-09-06 — see below.
- **The user can borrow, and it needed no new loan mechanic (`core/loanSearch.ts`, 2026-09-06).** `searchLoanTargets` browses, `requestLoan` acts, and a request that clears the gate goes straight through the existing `executeLoan` — same fee, same wage handoff, same transfer record, same automatic return. This is to `loans.ts` what `recommendations.ts` is to `negotiation.ts`. **Who is loanable is `runAILoanMarket`'s definition, mirrored, not a second one**: young (`LOAN_AI_MAX_AGE`), outside his club's XI, contract covers it, parent keeps its depth floor and `ROSTER_SAFETY_FLOOR`, and the borrower must clear the parent's `keepValueToClub` by `LOAN_MIN_SURPLUS`. Pure, **zero shared-`rng` draws** (the gate is deterministic — `valueToClub`, no jitter — which is also what keeps the list stable across renders), no persisted field, no migration. `LOAN_IN_MAX_PER_WINDOW` (2) is counted off `league.transfers`, so even the cap needed no state.
  - **The interesting result is that the market self-sorts by club strength, and it is emergent rather than tuned.** Measured on a fresh 626-club world, best borrowable OVR by the user's own tier: **tier 1 → 60, tier 2 → 76, tier 3 → 76**, against user XIs of 72.4 / 51.7 / 39.3. The cause is the buy-vs-keep asymmetry (`ValuationSide`): the buy side asks *how much would he improve us*, which is ~0 for a top-flight club and large for a lower-division one, while the parent's keep price is the same either way. So a big club can borrow nobody worth having and a small club can borrow players who walk into its eleven — which is what a loan is for in real football. `loanIn.test.ts` pins the ordering (tier 2 > tier 1), since an inversion would mean the loan market had become a way for big clubs to stockpile.
  - **The panel defaults to available-only, and that is the `forSaleOnly` lesson arriving again.** The **top 40 borrowable players by OVR are refused on price every time** — the best benched youngsters at big clubs are exactly the ones their clubs value most — so an unfiltered first view is 40 rows of "his club rates him too highly" and reads as a broken screen. Ranking is by OVR desc, so the toggle thins rows *before* the render cap. Cost is ~52ms on the 626-club world either way (dominated by the one `deriveLeagueContexts`, not by per-candidate gating), behind a debounce and a memo like the transfer search.
  - **The fee the USER pays is not `computeLoanFee` — it goes through `userLoanFee`, and both corrections were bugs found by a player asking "is this broken on brutal?" (2026-09-07).** Difficulty was *not* the cause and the fee was identical at all four levels; measuring that first is what found the real one. **(a) `trueTransferValue` returns exactly 0 at or below `VALUATION_OVR_FLOOR` (45), and that is 8,536 of 15,650 players — 55% of the world**, every one of them on a senior roster. Potential and age can't lift it: they are multipliers on a zero base. A loan fee is a fraction of that value, so **39 of a tier-1 user's 40 borrowable rows were free** and the column read "$0". `LOAN_FEE_MIN` (25k, × the duration multiplier) floors it. **(b) `buyPriceScale` reached `reservationPrice` and `scoutedValue` but never a loan fee**, so a loan was the one acquisition route that ignored the difficulty entirely — Brutal made buying 1.6x dearer and borrowing free. Both corrections are **user-path only**: `computeLoanFee` is shared with `runAILoanMarket` (~630 loans a season), so flooring or scaling it there moves real money between AI clubs and lands on the weak-league solvency tripwire — an audited change, where this is a price rule of the same shape as `buyPriceScale` itself. Measured after: zero free rows at any level, brutal 1.6x normal, and the quoted fee equals what the parent receives.
    - **The two corrections COMPOSE, and getting the order wrong makes the second one inert for most of the pool (2026-09-08, code review).** The first cut was `max(round(floor), round(computed * scale))` — scaling only the computed half. But the floor is exactly what binds for the ~55% of the world (a) is about, so every floored row read the same fee on Easy and Brutal, and difficulty was inert precisely where correction (a) had just taken effect, while the Manual and the changelog both said it applied. It is now `round(max(floor, computed) * scale)`: take the floor first, scale the whole range over the top of it. A floored player reads **$21,250 / $25,000 / $32,500 / $40,000** across easy→brutal, against a flat $25,000 before. **The existing difficulty test did not catch it and could not**, because it deliberately picked a player above the floor with the comment "or every level reads identically" — i.e. the bug was observed while writing the test and written down as expected behaviour rather than investigated. `loanIn.test.ts` now pins the floored case directly (verified failing without the fix: "expected 25000 to be less than 25000"). **The generalizable shape: when a floor and a multiplier are applied to the same quantity, the floor has to go underneath, or the multiplier stops reaching whatever the floor covers — and a test that steps around the boundary documents the bug instead of finding it.**
    - **Gate order is part of the copy, not just the logic.** The list is ranked by overall and its top rows are refused on price, so a user-state reason checked *last* never surfaces: a full squad read as forty clubs rating their players too highly. `hasRosterRoom` and the window cap are player-independent and now come **first**. Affordability can't be hoisted (it depends on the fee, hence the player), so a club in the red still can't surface it on a ranked list — the page states that once above the table instead. **The generalizable rule: in a ranked list of refusals, a reason that applies to every row must be checked before any per-row reason, or it is unreachable.**
    - **Hoisting the check is only half of it — each hoisted reason also needs its own panel-level alert, and the full squad shipped without one (2026-09-08, code review).** `availableOnly` defaults to **on**, so a condition that refuses every row empties the table rather than showing forty identical reasons, and the empty-state copy then reads "No clubs are willing to lend anyone right now" — blaming the AI clubs for something the user controls. There are exactly **three** such conditions and all three now have an alert: the window cap, a full roster, and being in the red. The cap alert's own comment already described this failure and the roster case slipped through beside it, which is the tell that the list is easy to leave incomplete: **whenever a gate gains a player-independent reason, it needs an alert in the same change.**
    - **Still open, deliberately untouched, and bigger than loans:** that 55% of the world values at exactly $0 is a property of the shipped valuation curve, and it reaches the permanent market too. Fixing it means moving `VALUATION_OVR_FLOOR` or the curve under it, which `scripts/marketRealismAudit.ts` gates and which would retune every fee in the game.
  - **No dynasty audit, and the reason is stronger than "it's user-only".** The youth-trialists entry audits a user-club-only change precisely because the user's club is in the league table, which is the one channel a user-only change leaks through. That argument does not reach here: `requestLoan` is only ever called from a click, so in a headless run — where the user's club is unmanaged by design — it never fires at all and `weakLeaguesAudit` would measure a world identical to main. Nothing in the offseason or the AI market path changed (`runAILoanMarket` is untouched and excluded the user on both sides already). An audit would be expensive *and* uninformative, which is a different verdict from expensive-but-skipped.
  - **No tier-2 ceiling guard for the user, deliberately, and it is reachable**: a tier-2 or tier-3 user club really can borrow a 76. That matches the buy side, which has never gated the user either (`divisionRefusalOvr` appears only in the *AI* buyer loops), and matches CLAUDE.md's standing rule that the user's club is exempt from `enforceDivisionCeilings` by design. Wages are the brake — the borrower pays them, and they are cubic in OVR.
  - **A user roster holding a player he does not own is a NEW STATE, and four user-only actions assumed ownership.** None was reachable before, because the user was never a loanee. All four now screen on "borrowed" (`loaneeTid === tid && parentTid !== tid`): `releasePlayer` (an optional trailing `activeLoans`, default empty so no existing caller moves — releasing a loanee would be a free early recall plus wage relief, and `processLoanReturns` would hand him back as if nothing had happened), `setTransferListed` and `inboundOfferCandidates` (which walks `user.roster` — an AI club would otherwise bid and the user could bank a fee for someone else's asset, orphaning the loan), `renewalsDue("senior")` (the senior *group is a roster*, i.e. who plays here, not who is owned here), and `extendContractAction`. The pages hide each control besides; the core refuses regardless. **The generalizable shape: `team.roster` answers "who plays for this club", never "who belongs to it", and every user action that spends or disposes of a player must ask the second question.**
    - **A fifth site was missed, and the rule now lives in ONE place (`core/loanOwnership.ts`, 2026-09-08, code review).** The Loans page's own "List a Player for Loan" table was built from `userTeam.roster` filtered on listings alone, so a player you had **in** on loan appeared there with a duration picker driven by *his parent club's* contract and a live button — which `listPlayerForLoan` then refuses (it returns the league unchanged for any pid already in `activeLoans`), i.e. a silent no-op on the very page the feature ships on, and exactly the shape the shared `loanGateFor` factory exists to prevent. Six sites were hand-rolling `loaneeTid === tid && parentTid !== tid` while `loanSearch.ts` exported an `isBorrowed` **nothing used** — because it took a `LeagueStore` and `releasePlayer` has only `teams`/`players`/`activeLoans`. Both helpers now take `ActiveLoan[]` and live in their own module. **It is its own file rather than `loans.ts` because of a cycle**: `loans.ts` imports `keepsDepthFloor` from `freeAgency.ts`, and `freeAgency.ts` is one of the callers, so a runtime import back would close the loop — `loanOwnership.ts` needs only the `ActiveLoan` *type*, so it sits under all of them. The two display sites (Roster's `borrowedFrom`, the Loans page's `inOnLoan`) still filter the rows themselves, since they need the parent club and return season rather than a pid set. **The lesson is about the dead export, not the duplication: a helper nobody can call because its signature doesn't fit its callers reads as coverage that isn't there — the rule looked centralised while six copies drifted.**
- **The Division-2 prevention guard was missing here until 2026-08-13, and was the *only* remaining way to breach the ceiling.** The buy paths (`transferMarket.ts`, `inboundOffers.ts`) both refuse to put an `ovr >= DIVISION_2_REFUSAL_OVR_THRESHOLD` player at a tier-2 club; `runAILoanMarket` never imported the constant. The function's own doc comment argued the restriction to buried under-24s made it impossible — "a 75+ prospect is starting somewhere" — but **the threshold is 70, not 75**, and a 70-73 under-24 outside a strong club's XI is ordinary. Measured (`scripts/loanCeilingProbe.ts`, 12 seasons, `userTid` excluded): **16 breaches, and 100% of every over-threshold tier-2 resident**, i.e. owned players never breach and this was the whole leak. **It is the worst place to have a hole**, because `enforceDivisionCeilings` cannot clean it — the sweep deliberately skips loaned pids (moving one leaves `processLoanReturns` handing a copy back to the parent, putting one pid on two rosters) and won't release a loaned-in player to make room — so each breach sits for the loan's full 1-3 seasons. Guard added in the buyer loop **after the jitter draw** (same RNG-stream-order rule as the market's identical guard); breaches → **0 in all 12 seasons**, loan volume unchanged (~630/season). Two general lessons, both cheap to repeat: **a doc comment asserting a guard is unnecessary is not a measurement** (this one and `LOAN_AVAILABILITY`'s were both wrong in the same file), and **when a mechanic's cleanup pass is forbidden to touch a category of state, prevention is the only option for that category.**
- **A loan now has to get the player a game, and that is the difference between the mechanic working and not (2026-09-07).** `runAILoanMarket` and `loanGateFor` both require `player.ovr > buyerCtx.posWeakestStarterOvr[player.pos]` — he must beat the weakest man the borrower's shape fields at his position. Measured with `scripts/loanMinutesProbe.ts` (seed 7, every summer loan followed through a full season at the borrowing club):

  | | before | after |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | loans agreed | 1,110 | 761 |
  | played at all | 47% | **89%** |
  | made the loanee's XI | 31% | **56%** |
  | appearances, median | **0** | **26** |
  | appearances, mean | 11.1 | 21.6 |

  Against a control of comparable u23 players who stayed home (5.9 mean appearances), a loan is now worth ~3.7x the minutes rather than ~1.7x. `posWeakestStarterOvr` reads 0 for a position the borrower is short of bodies at, so an outright hole always passes — right, since an empty slot is a guaranteed game. Placed **after the jitter draw** in the AI loop, like the tier-2 guard beside it, for the RNG-stream reason.
  - **The user's borrowing side takes the same rule, and it must.** Otherwise the user is offered deals no AI club could get, leaving the player as benched as he already was — the opposite of why his club agreed. It also does what an arbitrary minimum-value floor would have done to the list, on the principle instead of a number. In `loanGateFor` it sits **before** the price test, which is a copy decision rather than a behavioural one: a strong club fails both, and "He wouldn't get a game with you" is about the user's own squad and explains the short list, where "his club rates him too highly" is about somebody else and reads as bad luck.
  - **A club that is strong enough can now borrow nobody at all, and that is the mechanic, not a bug.** Measured across six clubs per tier on a fresh world, available rows per club: **tier 1 `0, 0, 28, 10, 40, 1`; tier 2 `40, 40, 40, 40, 1, 40`; tier 3 all 40.** It tracks club strength rather than tier — the very best sides are offered no one, a mid-table top-flight club gets a real list. **Test fixtures must therefore manage a second-division club**: `makeLeague(0, 1)` hands you tid 0, an English top-flight side that can borrow nobody, so a loan-in fixture built on it measures nothing (three test files were built on it and had to be repointed; same cached world, different `meta.userTid`, so no new fixture is generated).
  - **Audited: `weakLeaguesAudit.ts`, 20 seasons × 4 seeds, run per-seed on both sides and pooled by hand** (a single-seed invocation applies the script's *mean* gate to one draw and duly prints "BROKEN" on a converged rung — the documented misuse). **Ladder OK on every seed on both sides**, and pooled every rung is inside the ±1 tolerance on both. The headline spreads **widen**: BIG4→Serbia **9.65 → 11.03**, France→Serbia **5.92 → 7.12**. Resolvable rungs BIG4→France 3.73 → 3.91 and France→Netherlands 1.91 → 2.44; Netherlands→Portugal 2.37 → 2.12. The one rung to watch is **Greece→Scotland, +0.42 → −0.94**, which passes but sits against its −1 gate.
  - **Solvency is a genuine trade and worth stating plainly: same seed count, much smaller worst case, twice as many small dips.** Both sides run **3 of 4 seeds in deficit** — the baseline is red on this gate before the change, as it has been since #308, so do not read either side as green — but the worst case improves **−£11.4M → −£3.3M** while samples in deficit go **5/168 → 11/168**. Same shape the continental-prize change recorded: it neither fixes nor meaningfully worsens a pre-existing failure, it spreads a smaller total more thinly.
  - **The baseline for this audit was this branch itself, not `origin/main`**, which is the tighter comparison: everything else in the loan-in feature is user-path-only and never fires headlessly, so the two are identical to the audit and the A/B varies exactly one thing.
- **What that fixed, kept here because the measurement is the argument (`scripts/loanMinutesProbe.ts`, 2026-09-07):** more than half of all loans used to produce no minutes, which is the one thing a loan is for. A loan exists so a young player who isn't getting games gets them somewhere else — `minutesFactor` nudges progression during growth years, so a loan with no appearances has achieved nothing for anyone. Measured on seed 7 of the 626-club world, following **1,110 summer AI↔AI loans through a full season at the borrowing club**: **47% played at all, 31% made the loanee's XI, and the MEDIAN loanee played zero games.** It is still better than staying home — mean 11.1 appearances against **6.4** for comparable u23 players outside their own club's XI, so ~1.7x — so the mechanic helps; it just misses more often than it lands.
  - **The cause is a one-number mis-selection, and the two groups separate almost perfectly on it.** `runAILoanMarket` requires the borrower's `perceivedValueToClub` to clear the parent's keep price by `LOAN_MIN_SURPLUS`, and **"values him more" is not "will play him"**. Players who never played were a median **16 ovr BELOW the weakest man in the loanee's XI**; players who did play were **6 ABOVE**. Only **30 of 592** non-playing loanees were better than their new club's worst starter. So the market ships players to clubs that rate them without asking whether they'd get in the team.
  - **`loanOfferCandidates` takes the rule too, and that is the half the user cares about most**, since lending out is his own stated reason for the mechanic: a player good enough to develop but not good enough for his XI, sent somewhere he'll play. An offer from a club that would bench him looks like the deal he wanted and delivers nothing. Needs no audit — it only picks which AI club is offered for a player the user has already listed, and never runs headlessly. Consequence to know: **a weak enough listed player may now draw no offers at all**, which is the honest answer rather than a broken one. Note the probe measures AI↔AI loans, so the size of the effect on this path is **reasoning, not a measurement**.
- **`LOAN_AVAILABILITY` is gone (2026-08-13) and the loan market was nearly a dead mechanic without knowing it.** The screen skipped any player whose keep-value to his parent exceeded 0.95 × his market value — the same club-relative-vs-club-blind category error that `AI_MARKET_AVAILABILITY` made, kept deliberately when that one was deleted so as not to move two markets at once, with a comment conceding its justification was "reasoning, not a measurement". Measured, it was binding: it excluded **~71% of clubs' best loan-eligible players** (`scripts/loanAvailabilityProbe.ts`, median keep/market 1.30). **Volume is the wrong metric here and this is the durable lesson: the loan market is cap-bound (`AI_LOAN_MAX_MOVES`), not screen-bound**, so removing a filter that excluded 71% of *candidates* left executed loans flat at ~635/season — it changes *which* players move, not how many. On quality (`scripts/loanCeilingProbe.ts`, 12 seasons): loans involving an ovr-65+ player went **4.3 → 29.7 per season (6.9×)**, mean loaned ovr 38.8 → 41.1. Four useful loans a season across 320 clubs meant the screen was filtering out exactly the rated prospects a loan exists to serve, leaving only players nobody wanted. Protection is by price alone now (`LOAN_MIN_SURPLUS`: the borrower must value him above his parent's reservation). **The risk this created is on the wage channel, not the receipts one** — wages key off roster membership, so a loan moves salary to the borrower, and 6.9× more well-paid players moving redistributes materially more wage bill toward the (often weaker) borrowing clubs; that is what the ladder/solvency audit gates, not the transfer-receipts table.

- **Contract expiry and loans collided, and the offseason order is now load-bearing (2026-08-18).** A loan deliberately leaves the contract alone — it travels with the player and comes home with him — so nothing reconciled the two, and `releaseExpiredContracts` (step 1) ran *before* `processLoanReturns` (step 1.5). A loaned player sits on the **loanee's** roster, so expiry dropped him *there*: he ended up on no roster at all while `freeAgentPids` still counted him as rostered (he was on loan), i.e. unplayable, unsignable and invisible, and was then handed back to his parent carrying an already-dead contract. `runAILoanMarket` screened on age and "outside the XI" but **not on contract status**, so it could loan him straight back out and restart the loop. Measured across six real player saves (`docs/player-save-findings.md` #2): **71-281 stale contracts each, 100% of them loan returns**, worst six seasons past expiry; ages 19-24, median ovr 38 — exactly the loaned-out population. Knock-ons were all downstream of the dead contract: `departsAtRollover` true forever so the market would never buy him, `trueTransferValue` pinned at the minimum contract multiplier, a permanent "Final year" badge. **No money hole** — wages charge off roster membership, so this never touched finance, which is why nothing caught it. Fixed as all three of the shapes the findings doc listed, because each closes a different half: **(1)** returns run **before** expiry, so he lands home first and his contract is settled there — released into free agency in the same rollover, in time for step 4 to sign him; **(2)** expiry **skips pids still out on loan** (`onLoanPids`), or a multi-season loan strands him again; **(3)** a loan whose player's contract has expired is **terminated at step 1.25** rather than run on, so he can't sit out the rest of it on a dead deal — only reachable on saves predating (4). **(4)** `maxLoanSeasons`/`loanOutlivesContract` cap a new loan at the seasons his deal covers (the Loans page's duration picker offers no more, `listPlayerForLoan` and `loanOfferCandidates` refuse the rest, and `runAILoanMarket` skips an expired contract — inert going forward since AI loans are 1 season, and it is what repairs an old save's loop). **Renewals now follow ownership, not roster membership** (`runAIContractRenewals` takes `activeLoans`): the club **borrowing** a player used to be the one deciding his contract, which is what rescued the other ~73% of loan returns and hid the bug's true size, while his actual owner — who cannot see him on any roster — had no way to renew him at all. The user is affected identically and had it worst (his club is skipped by AI renewals entirely, so *nothing* could rescue his loaned-out player), hence the **Extend control on the Loans page's "Players Out on Loan" list** — the only surface where he appears, since a loaned-out player is not on the Roster page. `extendContractAction` resolves the **parent** club for `wouldRefuseExtension`, not whichever roster holds the pid. **This reorders the offseason, so it is rng-load-bearing** — a player released or not released shifts every downstream draw; the dynasty is re-baselined and single-season metric moves are noise until re-measured over ≥15 seasons. Verify with `scripts/loanContractProbe.ts` (stale + limbo counts per season, exits non-zero if either is ever above 0); `scripts/playerSaveAudit.ts` reports the same thing on a real save.

**Scouting fog-of-war on potential** (`core/scouting/potentialFog.ts`)
- Fogs **POT only** (OVR/attributes stay exact) as a low-high range, narrowed by scouting spend + ownership tenure, clearing to exact over ~2-3 seasons. `StoredTeam.scoutingObserved` (pid→first-seen season, user only, reconciled at league creation + each offseason). `<PotDisplay>` replaces every raw potential render; AI clubs unaffected (they have their own `perceivedValueToClub` noise). Team-level aggregates stay exact.
- **Scouting spend is locked per season** — `StoredTeam.nextScoutingSpend` (editable only in offseason) becomes the committed `scoutingSpend` at rollover, closing a peek-then-lower exploit.

**Career charts** (`ui/components/ValueHistoryChart.tsx`, `OvrHistoryChart.tsx`, shared `chartColors.ts`)
- **Transfer-value chart** on the **active** Player Profile (2026-08-12, replaced the OVR chart there): value on y, season on x, area colored by the club that season, crests at transfers, hover card with value / age / OVR / fogged POT / G-A / appearances / avg match rating / club. A **Value/OVR pill toggle** (`careerChart` state) swaps the same panel to `OvrHistoryChart` — kept because the two genuinely diverge (a veteran's rating holds flat while the age curve guts his value). **Retired** profiles get `OvrHistoryChart` alone (a retiree has no market value, and `ArchivedPlayer` carries no potential or contract to price one from).
- Crest tooltips on **both** charts go through `transferFeeLabel` — a bare `fee > 0` test rendered a loan and a loan return as "(free)", i.e. exactly the confusion that helper exists to prevent (pre-existing bug, fixed 2026-08-12).
- **Derived, not stored** (`core/finance/valueHistory.ts` `careerValueHistory`) — replays `trueTransferValue` over `player.hist`, so no schema change and existing saves chart their whole past immediately.
- **Season offset is load-bearing.** A `RatingsSnapshot` is stamped at the *end* of a season, so the one labelled N is what he carried through **N+1** — the same convention `awards.ts`'s `ovrDuringSeason` reads and `archive.ts` already bakes into `ArchivedSeason`. Points plot at `snap.season + 1`, which is also what makes the final point exactly his value today. The Profile's OVR/POT/attribute *table* deliberately keeps labelling rows by the stamp season (its footnote says so), so table and chart legitimately differ by one label.
- **`RatingsSnapshot.academy` does NOT take that offset** — progression reads it for the season that just *ended*, so it describes season N while the ratings beside it describe N+1. Shifting it would label a promoted youngster's first senior year "(Academy)". `careerValueHistory` therefore drops it; `PlayerProfile` resolves it per played season (`academyBySeason`, falling back to live squad membership for the current season, which has no snapshot yet). Caught in runtime verification, pinned by a test.
- **Contract term is held flat** at the player's *present* years-remaining for every point — old contract lengths aren't stored, so this keeps the curve a read on ability/age/potential (one constant multiplier) instead of distorting it with a guess.
- **POT goes through the scouting fog before pricing** (`pricedPotential` in `PlayerProfile`), or the dollar figure becomes an exact read on a hidden number (`trueTransferValue` pays a potential-gap premium). The fog band is taken once at `league.season` so its seeded jitter is one constant and the line stays smooth; the hover card still quotes each season's *own* band, matching the table.
- **One real point per season, drawn as a curve.** Ratings only move in the offseason, so there is no intra-season measurement — the line is a Fritsch–Carlson monotone cubic (no overshoot, can't draw a value he never had or dip below zero) sampled `SAMPLES_PER_SEASON` times and split into club-colored runs by nearest season. Hover always snaps to a season. y-axis is zero-based (money).

**Retirement** (`retirementProbability`/`isWantedForRetirement`, `players/progression.ts`; offseason step 3)
- **Age sets the curve's shape, last season's roster status sets its scale** (2026-07-28 rework; was age-only, identical odds for a 33-year-old star and a 33-year-old benchwarmer). Rostered → age curve × `RETIREMENT_ROSTERED_DAMPING` (damped, never zeroed: age still dominates). Unrostered → `RETIREMENT_UNROSTERED_BASE` at *any* age plus the undamped curve, so unsigned players drift out instead of sitting in the pool from 16 until 33. **Exception:** under `RETIREMENT_PROSPECT_MAX_AGE` **and** potential > `RETIREMENT_PROSPECT_POT_THRESHOLD` falls back to the damped curve, so high-ceiling kids between clubs aren't washed out.
- **The exemption's age bound is load-bearing.** `estimatePotential` seeds its peak at current ovr and only raises it, so `potential >= ovr` always. Without an age gate the exemption catches *every* unrostered player above ovr 65 — a released 37-year-old included — and hands him the damped curve (0.318 at 37 vs 0.53 under the old age-only model), i.e. it would make good unsigned veterans retire ~40% *less* than before the rework, and leave the ovr-66+ slice of the pool as the one population nothing removes (the cull spares it too, on `FREE_AGENT_CULL_MAX_PEAK_OVR`). Caught in review; a test pins it.
- **Both exemption bounds are pinned to the pool cull's constants by reference, not copied** (`RETIREMENT_PROSPECT_POT_THRESHOLD` = `FREE_AGENT_CULL_MAX_POT`, `RETIREMENT_PROSPECT_MAX_AGE` = `FREE_AGENT_CULL_MIN_AGE`), so the pair can't drift apart on either shared axis. They are **not** fully complementary: the cull also spares career peak > `FREE_AGENT_CULL_MAX_PEAK_OVR`, which retirement ignores on purpose.
- **"Rostered" means *last season*, snapshotted before step 1**, because retirement is step 3, step 1 has already released every expired contract into the free pool, and AI free agency doesn't re-sign anyone until step 4 — a live read retires a crowd of players about to be picked back up. Uses `freeAgentPids`, so academy players and players out on loan count as rostered.
- **Known gap in that grace:** it covers contract expiry (step 1, before free agency at step 4) but *not* mid-offseason releases. `trimRosterSurplus` (step 6) and `enforceDivisionCeilings`'s weakest-player release both run *after* step 4, and `runAIFreeAgency` is offseason-only, so a trimmed player's first free agency is the next offseason's step 4 — which falls after his step-3 retirement roll. He is rolled once without ever having been offered. Fixing it properly means reordering the offseason (RNG-order consequences), so it is documented rather than patched; empirically it costs nothing measurable (min AI roster size *improved* and D1 mean ovr held).
- **One `rng()` draw per player either way**, so the shared stream's draw *count* is unchanged (outcomes move, which is the point). Retirees are stripped from `academyRoster` as well as `roster` — defence-in-depth only, still unreachable, since `freeAgentPids` counts academy players as rostered and the damped curve is exactly 0 below `RETIREMENT_START_AGE`.
- **Retirement scrubs live references but keeps history.** Retirees are dropped from `activeLoans`, `negotiations`, `inboundOffers`, `loanListings`, `loanRejections` — state pointing at a player who no longer exists (an un-scrubbed `activeLoan` would have `processLoanReturns` hand a dead pid back to its parent and log a phantom transfer). `transfers`/`newsEvents`/cup box scores are deliberately **kept**: unlike a culled nobody, a retiree had a real career. **Pre-existing consequence, made more frequent by this change:** those rows render as "Player &lt;pid&gt;" once he's deleted, and `awardedPids` protection (which the cull honours) does not apply to retirement, so an award winner who retires can leave an honours-board slot resolving to `undefined`. Both predate this rework; the honest fix is a retired-player archive, which is a design call.
- **Retirees are snapshotted onto the season-history entry, because they're deleted** (`core/players/retirements.ts`, `SeasonHistoryEntry.retirements?: RetirementSummary`, rendered as the Season Preview's Retirements section). `summarizeRetirements` copies name/nationality/pos/age/ovr/last club/career line/caps — a *copy*, not a pid reference, since the pid resolves to nothing a moment later (the same reason his surviving transfer rows read "Player &lt;pid&gt;"). Four gotchas: **(a)** the last-club map (`tidLastSeason` in `offseason.ts`) is built from the **pre-step-1** rosters, alongside the `unrosteredLastSeason` snapshot and for the identical reason — a live read files a fifteen-year club legend whose contract just expired as a free agent on his way out. **(b)** `notable` is capped at `RETIREMENT_NOTABLE_LIMIT` (15) and this is a *save-size* cap as much as a DOM cap: the list is persisted per season and thousands of unsigned players now retire per offseason, so an uncapped list is the 88 MB save and the `/transfers` freeze at once. `total`/`rostered` carry the true numbers. The user's own retirees are never dropped (same guarantee `WINDOW_TRANSFER_LIMIT` makes about his own deals), but they still take their place by rank rather than being pinned top. **(c) Which retirees make the cap, and their order, is the GOAT score, not the rating each retired at (2026-09-01, user call).** A rating is a snapshot of a player at his *weakest* — a great career ends declining — so ovr ranked a twelve-year, three-Ballon-d'Or forward bowing out at 71 below whichever unsigned 24-year-old drifted out at 74, which is the exact inverse of "the biggest names to go". `summarizeRetirements` now takes the league and scores each retiree through `goatScores` (`frivolities/goat.ts`), with final ovr surviving as the first tiebreak so two players with nothing on their record still come out sensibly. **Two things make it work at the moment of retirement.** `archivePlayer` already reduces a live retiree to an `ArchivedPlayer` off his *stored career summary*, and `rowFromArchived` (newly exported) turns that into the `CareerRow` the scorer wants — so a retiree is scored without walking his season lines, which by then may be nothing but the worker-side window (see `careerSummary.ts`); and it is **not** the archive, which most careers never enter. **And it runs at offseason step 3.66, not step 3.** Honours are read off `seasonHistory` and the cup histories, and at step 3 the season just finished has reached neither — scoring there would dock every retiree exactly the trophies he went out on, which is GOAT gotcha (c) arriving from a new direction. So 3.66 assembles the `SeasonHistoryEntry` *once* (it was previously built inline in the return) and the farewell list is scored against a league view carrying it plus the still-live `cup`/`shield`/`domesticCups`; the return then spreads that same entry, so the scored view and the stored one cannot drift. Pure, **zero rng draws**, so dynasties are bit-identical. **It reads the cup histories, which the WORKER DOES NOT HAVE, and that shipped broken for a few hours (2026-09-02).** `detachNews` hands the worker empty `cupHistory`/`shieldHistory`/`domesticCupHistory`, and the offseason runs in that worker — so scoring a retiree there credited him with none of the cups he had ever won: no crash, no type error, just a wrong ranking, which is verbatim the failure mode `simArchive.ts` documents. `simArchive.test.ts`'s deep-equality gate caught it, in CI, after it had been judged "low risk" and skipped locally. Fixed the way `referencedPids` already solves this exact boundary: `computeHonours` now takes an explicit **`HonourSources`** (`seasonHistory` plus `{season, championTid}` projections of the three cups) rather than a `LeagueStore`, the main thread computes it off the full league before detaching and passes it through the protocol as `OffseasonInputs.cupChampions`, and a direct caller (every test, script, and `jump`, which is exempt from detaching) falls back to reading the league. **Three things about the shape are the point.** The projection is two numbers per cup per season — a few KB per century against the ~23 MB `detachNews` exists to remove — so correctness costs nothing the detach was buying. Swapping `LeagueStore` for a named source object breaks every caller **at compile time**, which is what a silent boundary needs, and it let the retirement test drop its `as unknown as LeagueStore` cast. And **the gate hand-models the boundary rather than going through `useSimWorker`**, so threading the input through the app was necessary and not sufficient: the test passed only once its model handed in the same two precomputed values the real caller does. Anything added to the offseason that reads a detached field has to do all three. **The score IS persisted, on `RetiredPlayer.goat`, and it is the one GOAT score in the game that isn't derived** — it is shown as a column on the Season Preview, and the player is deleted moments after the row is written, so by render time there is no career left to score. That also makes it a reading taken *at retirement*: later seasons cannot change it, which is what a farewell list should say anyway. Optional and **never backfilled** (same one-way door as `retirements` itself: an older list names players nothing can score), so the column is blank on old seasons and `byCaliber` reads the field rather than a lookup — a list re-sorted after storage comes out in the order it is already in instead of collapsing back to ovr. **(d)** The field is **optional and deliberately never backfilled** — a season that ran before the record existed deleted its retirees without a trace, so `migrate.ts` leaves it absent and the page says so rather than inventing a list. Also: no `/player/<pid>` link on those rows, the profile would be empty.
- Audited with `scripts/retirementAudit.ts` (pool size, **min AI roster size** — the real safety metric, since retirement runs immediately before AI free agency draws from the pool — D1 mean ovr, squad age, count still active at 38+). Measured vs an origin/main baseline, same seed, at season 13: free-agent pool 5194-and-still-climbing → **2136 and plateaued** from season 9, players still active at 38+ 24 → **71**, min AI roster 18 → **22**, D1 mean ovr **55.6 → 55.7 (unchanged)**. **The prospect exemption is what holds that last number**: without it D1 mean fell to 52.4, because washing out young FAs starved AI free agency of the useful under-24s it fills holes with, leaving barbell squads of teenagers and veterans. Any future move on `RETIREMENT_UNROSTERED_BASE` or the exemption threshold must re-check D1 mean ovr against a baseline, not just pool size.

**Extremism** (occasional extreme outcomes without reopening inflation)
- **Generational talents** — `isGenerational(pid)` (`players/progression.ts`), ~1/2500 per pid, softer growth-damping + floored bias → a true 90+ legend ~once/30 seasons. **Deliberately unannounced and unbadged (2026-08-17, user call).** The arrival used to fire a `generationalTalent` News Feed event; that event type is **deleted**, and `migrate.ts` strips it from saves in progress (an append-only feed would otherwise keep leaking it forever). Two reasons, both worth keeping: (a) the trait is hidden by design and the only intended tell is a scout's unusually high `estimatePotential` ceiling, so announcing it handed the user a hidden number; (b) **the flag is read from a pid hash before ratings exist**, so it can land on a player who is structurally incapable of ever being good — a real user save had an announced "generational talent" at **OVR 9 / POT 51-55**. Note the perk that makes the name mean anything (relaxed damping) is **inert below `GROWTH_DAMPING_START` (65)**, so on a weak prospect only the floored bias applies and the flag is worth ~+25 potential, not the ~+80 it is worth to a healthy academy prospect (measured: broken kid POT 35→61, healthy kid 60-69→82-87). Don't re-add an announcement without gating it on the player actually having a ceiling.
- **Historic team seasons** — `core/teamSeasonForm.ts`, hidden ±season-long form swing (1.5% each way), applied to composites in `simThrough` (cup too). On-pitch only (no market side effects). Both derived from hashes (no shared-rng consumption). User's club eligible symmetrically.
