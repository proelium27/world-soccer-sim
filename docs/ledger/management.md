# Manager career, national team job, roster & tactics, autopilot, spectator, God Mode

> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-25 to keep session context small. This is the detailed ledger for this area: read it before touching the code it describes, and add new entries here, not to CLAUDE.md. References to "this file" below meant CLAUDE.md as a whole; the other sections now live alongside this one in `docs/ledger/`.

**Manager career (the user's club can change mid-save)**
- **`meta.userTid` is no longer fixed for the life of a save (2026-08-23).** `core/manager/` adds board confidence, job offers and sackings, all resolved at the offseason boundary. The reassignment itself was always trivial — every one of the ~198 `userTid` consumers reads it live, so the AI starts managing the club you left and stops managing the one you joined on the next read. **The load-bearing part is `switchClub`'s handover**, because several `StoredTeam` fields are *user-only by convention* and an AI club that inherits one populated is in a state nothing unwinds.
  - **The academy is the one that silently breaks things.** `academyRoster` is a user-only holding pool: AI clubs never promote from it, never field those players and never release them — yet the offseason's season-start charge sums `[...roster, ...academyRoster]` and `freeAgentCull` counts them as rostered. Left behind they are permanent zombies: paid, uncullable, unable to ever play again. `switchClub` graduates them onto the senior roster on ordinary `contractTerms` (rng-free), letting the next `trimRosterSurplus` sort out the surplus.
  - Also cleared on the way out: `starters`→null, `transferListed`/`moreMinutes`/`scoutingObserved`→empty, `nextScoutingSpend`=`scoutingSpend`, plus **only that club's** formation re-picked via `chooseBestFormation`. Re-running `assignAIFormations` over the world instead would re-pick 300-odd clubs' shapes at a moment nothing else in the sim does, changing their match results as a side effect of the user changing jobs. `ensureUserRosterSafety` runs once on the departing club, since it only ever protects `userTid`. League-level `negotiations`/`inboundOffers`/`loanListings`/`loanRejections` are dropped (all implicitly "the user's"); `activeLoans` deliberately survive (club-owned contracts).
  - **Arriving fogs the new squad** (`reconcileScoutingObserved({}, roster, season)`) — falls out of the existing scouting model rather than being special-cased, and reads exactly right: you know their ratings, not their ceilings.
  - **Runs at the offseason transition in `simThrough`, beside `initInternationalCampaign`, not inside `simOffseason`.** It judges the season just finished (wants that final table and nothing after it), and it must be answerable *before* the offseason runs so a manager who moves manages the **new** club's summer. Pure state, **no shared-`rng` draw** — offers use their own `hashInts` stream (`MANAGER_OFFER_STREAM` 970), so the `touchStats` scoreline hash and the M1 standings gate are untouched (verified).
  - **Confidence is a running 0-100 balance, not a "last N seasons" rule** — legible to the user, and it lets goodwill be *banked*, so an overachiever survives one bad year and relegation is never an automatic sacking. Judged on **finish vs the club's standing**, never vs its current squad — see the next bullet. Deliberately **not** built on `deriveLeagueContexts`'s `stature`: that is the transfer AI's view, and coupling to it would let a market retune silently change who gets sacked.
  - **Expectations must not be settable by the manager, and the first model got this wrong (user catch, 2026-08-23).** `deriveExpectations` originally ranked clubs by `computeTeamRating`. Squad quality is the one variable the manager fully controls, so tearing the team down lowered the bar with it: sell everyone, finish next-to-last with the worst squad in the league, and the board reads it as *beating expectations*. It rewarded sabotage. **Standing is now built only from what a transfer window cannot touch** — recent finishes (`MANAGER_EXPECTATION_W_HISTORY` 0.7, dominant because they are the most direct statement of what a club is) and hype (0.3), each normalized within the competition. **There is deliberately no money term, and two attempts at one were removed** (code review, 2026-08-23): bank balance alone is *backwards*, since spending the kitty is the normal thing a manager does and emptying it moved a mid-table club's bar six places easier while it fielded a stronger squad; balance-plus-wage-bill fixes buying and selling (each just moves value between the halves) but not **releasing**, where wages fall with no fee arriving, so a teardown still dragged the bar down — caught by the teardown test itself. Self-harming is not the same as impossible, and the model exists so that *no* sequence of transfer decisions moves the bar. Cost: a suddenly-rich club is not expected to improve until it actually does, which is the right way round for a board that judges results. Squad rating survives only as the season-1 bootstrap, blended out by `historyCoverage` as real results accumulate — and that is the squad the manager was *handed*. Two traps: **(a)** a past finish is meaningless without its division, so `seasonStanding` converts it to a percentile and lays the divisions on **one continuous scale meeting at seams of `MANAGER_EXPECTATION_TIER_SEAM` (0.35)** — tier 1 takes [seam, 1] and each tier below takes a band of width `seam` under it, so tier 2 is [0, seam] and tier 3 is **[-seam, 0]** — or a promoted second-division champion carries a "1st" into the top flight and is expected to win it. **Going NEGATIVE is deliberate and is what let a third division be seated without retuning the two above it**: the sole consumer feeds this through `normalizeWithinComp`, which min-max rescales each competition against its own observed range, so nothing reads the absolute value and the scale was never bounded to [0, 1]. Tiers 1 and 2 are byte-identical as a function to the two-tier version. **The bug this replaces was `tier === 2 ? low : high`, which sent every OTHER tier into the top-flight band** — measured, a third-division title scored **1.000, identical to winning the top flight**, and finishing bottom of the third division outscored mid-table in the second, so a third-division manager was judged on top-flight standards. It needs no dynasty audit for the same reason the difficulty levers don't: `deriveExpectations` is read only for `expectations.get(userTid)`, so nothing reaches the AI or the rng stream. `scripts/managerTenureProbe.ts` is still the calibration tool if the bands are ever moved; **(b)** `reviewSeason` must pass `league.seasonHistory` *excluding the season being judged* — it gets this for free since a season is appended during the following offseason, but any new caller must preserve it, or the bar moves to match the result it is judging. `test/core/manager.test.ts` pins the teardown case directly.
  - **Playoff leagues are judged on the playoff run (`core/manager/playoffExpectation.ts`, 2026-09-22, user ask).** Where `competitionTitlePlayoff` isn't "none" and the club's playoff is decided, `judgePlayoffSeason` puts both sides of the verdict on the playoff bracket: an expected rank inside the places becomes a stage (rank 1 = win it, 2 = final, 3-4 = semis/conference finals, …, `playoffStages` derives the bands from the format's round count and entrant count), and a club that made it is scored on the middle of the places its exit shares (QF exit = 6.5). A club expected outside the places keeps its table expectation, and one that missed keeps its table finish but never better than the last seat. `judgeSeason` just swaps those two numbers in, so demand, patience and swing are unchanged, plus a flat `MANAGER_MISSED_PLAYOFFS_CONFIDENCE` (-5) for every club that misses (sized so a club that always misses settles around 23, not the sack). Table leagues are byte-identical (`verdict.playoff` absent); the verdict's optional `playoff` needs no migration. Deliberately NOT applied to `deriveExpectations`' history term: past seasons still feed the bar by table position, so a club's standing is unchanged by this. Nothing reaches the AI or the rng stream.
  - **Continental football is judged too (`core/manager/continentalExpectation.ts`, 2026-09-22, user ask).** Two terms in `judgeSeason`. **Qualifying**: the expected competition is where `expectedRank` lands in the league's standing slot counts (region's top competition = 2 rungs, the one below = 1, so the Americas Cup is 2), the earned one comes off `qualificationByTid` with this season's routes (domestic cup winners, holders); `MANAGER_CONTINENTAL_PLACE_CONFIDENCE` (5) per rung, flat. **The run**: a club in a finished Swiss/custom competition is expected to reach the stage its draw seed (`cup.seeds`, set from last season's tables, so transfer-proof) falls in; exit and expectation become places on the field (`cupStages`, sized off the cup itself), scored like the league at `MANAGER_CONTINENTAL_RUN_SWING` (25) with the same demand asymmetry. **It deliberately reads NO cup history**: `reviewSeason` runs in the sim worker, which `detachNews` hands empty cup archives, so qualification ignores the rolling-coefficient reallocation (it only differs by one place at the cut, and only where a country moved a place). Legacy straight-bracket cups have no seeds and are skipped. Both verdict fields optional, no migration.
  - **Harshness has two axes.** In-world `demand` blends club-rank-within-league with league-strength-within-world (asymmetric: a demanding board damps rewards and amplifies penalties). Globally, **`DifficultyProfile.boardPatience`** (easy 1.7 / normal 1 / hard 0.75 / brutal 0.55) scales the whole verdict — the one difficulty knob with **no world effect**, so it needs no `difficultyProbe` recalibration.
  - **`MANAGER_CONFIDENCE_SWING` was sized against measured variance, and the first guess was 1.9x too harsh.** `scripts/managerTenureProbe.ts` judges **every AI club as if it were the user** over N seasons and reports sackings per 100 club-seasons per difficulty. Judging the *actual* user club is useless here: it is unmanaged in a headless run, so it rots and would report "the board fires everyone" against any tuning whatsoever. Measured over 2552 club-seasons: an ordinary season lands within **±0.158 of a division** (p25-p75, ~±3 places in a 20-club league), a bad one about **-0.368** (p10). At the first-guess swing of 130 a single p10 season cost ~68 against a starting 65 — one bottom-decile year all but ended a career — and normal difficulty sacked a manager every 8 seasons (12.5 per 100 club-seasons, median tenure 3, only 119/320 surviving 8 seasons). Now 70. Re-measured after expectations moved onto club standing (which is noisier than squad rating, since results vary more than ratings do — p10 -0.42 vs -0.37): normal lands at **7.6 per 100 club-seasons — one sacking per ~13 seasons**, median tenure 5, 163/320 surviving 8 seasons; easy 2.3, hard 11.5, brutal 15.0. (Dropping the money term moved these by under 0.2 — it was contributing almost nothing to the distribution while offering an exploit channel, so removing it was free.) Re-run the probe after touching either constant or any `boardPatience`; note the figures describe **competently-managed AI clubs**, so they are the baseline a human over- or under-performs against, not a prediction of one player's tenure.
  - **`MANAGER_CONFIDENCE_RECOVERY` (0.12) exists because the probe exposed a dead end, not to soften the numbers.** Confidence only ever moved on over/underperformance, so a manager at 20 who then finished *exactly* to expectation every season stayed at 20 forever, permanently one bad year from the sack with no route back. Each season now drifts 12% of the way back toward `MANAGER_START_CONFIDENCE` **before** the verdict, which cuts both ways on purpose: a long-banked 100 also decays, so a title six years ago stops being a permanent shield.
  - **The offer pool was the intersection of an ABSOLUTE band and a RELATIVE filter, and at the top of either scale they stopped overlapping (2026-09-02, user report).** `generateJobOffers` centred a `MANAGER_OFFER_BAND`-wide window on `reputation/100` — an absolute position on the world prestige scale — and then intersected it with `prestige >= currentPrestige`, which is relative to the job you hold. The pool is empty whenever `currentPrestige > reputation/100 + MANAGER_OFFER_BAND`, and that is reachable two ways which look unrelated and are the same arithmetic: a **decorated** manager who climbed to the summit (reputation caps at 100, so the band caps at 1.2, while `prestige` is min-max normalized and hands exactly one club 1.000 — no club can ever satisfy the step-up filter) and an **unproven** one handed a big club (base reputation 30 puts the band's top at 0.5, below any serious club). Measured on a fresh 626-club world at reputation 100, mean offers per season by the user club's world rank: **rank 1 `0.00`, rank 3 `0.75`, rank 10 `2.83`, rank 25 `3.33`** — i.e. the better you did, the fewer offers you got, until they stopped. Fixed by centring on `max(reputation/100, currentPrestige)` and allowing a `MANAGER_OFFER_LATERAL_BAND` (0.05) step down, so **your current club sets the floor and reputation sets how far above it you can reach**. Reputation still does the only job it had — it is what lets a small club's manager be offered the biggest jobs in the world — it simply no longer *caps* how big a job may approach you.
    - **`generateNationOffers` had it word for word** and took the identical fix (`NATIONAL_OFFER_LATERAL_BAND` 0.08, wider because national prestige is read off *rank* over ~44 nations, so one place is ~0.023 and 0.05 would reach barely two). The unemployed path is **not** affected by this fix and needed its own — `currentPrestige` is 0 with no job, so the `max` is inert there; see `NATIONAL_OFFER_CLUB_REP_WEIGHT` immediately below.
    - **A club career was INVISIBLE to federations, which made the unemployed path a dead end (2026-09-02, user question, `NATIONAL_OFFER_CLUB_REP_WEIGHT` 0.65).** A separate bug from the band/filter one above and found by asking about the case that fix does *not* touch: with no country, `currentPrestige` is 0 so the `max` is inert. `nationalReputation` is derived from `NationalStint[]` alone, and the only way to open a stint is to be offered a job, so a club manager who has never held one is pinned at `NATIONAL_REP_BASE` (30) **forever**. Measured on the real 70-nation world: 4 offers every offseason (so the path was never *dead*), but always from **ranks #45-62**, and the best country the band could reach at all was **#33** — identical at club reputation 30 and 100. A decorated club career bought literally nothing, which is not how the crossover works in real football. Federations now see a discounted slice of `managerReputation`, folded into the same offer target.
      - **It feeds the OFFER TARGET ONLY, never `nationalReputation` itself.** That number is displayed to the player as their international standing and must keep meaning what it says; it also drives nothing else, so confidence and sackings stay strictly on the national record and a club-side retune still cannot change who a federation dismisses — the property `NATIONAL_*` exists as a second constant block to protect. `clubReputation` is a **required** field on `NationOfferInputs` rather than an optional one, so a new caller breaks at compile time instead of silently under-offering.
      - **Swept against what is ACTUALLY OFFERED, not against the band arithmetic, and the two disagree by ten places.** The band's ceiling at target `t` is `t + NATIONAL_OFFER_BAND`, but `pool.slice(0, NATIONAL_MAX_OFFERS * 4)` then keeps only the 16 nations *closest to the target*, truncating the top of the band away. At weight 0.5 the band reaches rank 18 and the best country ever offered over 20 seasons is **rank 28**. The first draft of this entry quoted the reachable number as though it were the offered one. Measured best-offered at club reputation 100, by weight: 0.50 → #28, 0.55 → #25, 0.60 → #21, **0.65 → #18 (Wales)**, 0.70 → #14, 0.75 → #11 (Brazil). **Quote the offered rank; the reachable one is the optimistic lie.**
      - **The ladder is preserved deliberately and is pinned by a test.** The top five nations stay out of reach on a club career alone; Spain still needs a national reputation near 75, earned in the job. The weight also only bites above club reputation **47** (below that it lands under `NATIONAL_REP_BASE` and the `max` ignores it), so an unproven manager's offers are byte-identical to before and roughly one league title is what starts opening it.
      - The probe's national grid is a **synthetic 44-nation field** and so reports a *different* rank for the same weight (#9 rather than #18). It is built rather than simmed because the arithmetic is what is being probed, but it means **the shipped calibration numbers came from a real world and must be re-measured there**, not read off the probe.
    - **The sacked path took the same centring, and that IS a behaviour change** — `MANAGER_SACKED_PRESTIGE_PENALTY` means "a rung down", which only means something relative to where you were; measuring the drop from *reputation* was the same category error. A manager sacked by a giant is no longer offered the bottom of the pyramid. It was never *broken* there (the path has a never-empty fallback), which is exactly why it would have been left carrying the bug.
    - **Count alone cannot detect this and cannot verify the fix, which is why `scripts/managerOfferProbe.ts` prints two grids.** Offer count saturates near `MANAGER_MAX_OFFERS` once the pool is comfortably large, so after the fix every cell reads ~3.33 and reputation looks inert. It isn't: the second grid is the mean prestige of the clubs offered, where a prestige-0.349 club's manager goes **0.346 at reputation 30 to 0.960 at 100**. Read them together.
    - **Nothing here touches the sim.** Both lists draw on their own seeded streams (`MANAGER_OFFER_STREAM` 970 / `NATIONAL_OFFER_STREAM` 971) and nothing else reads an offer, so no scoreline, rating or dynasty baseline moves and no audit was needed — only the number of draws *within* those two streams changes.
    - **The generalizable lesson, and it is the fifth instance of this shape in this file: a filter expressed in absolute units and a filter expressed relative to the user are not composable, and their intersection fails silently at the extremes.** It presents as a dead feature rather than an error — no throw, no empty-state copy, just a list that is always empty for the players most likely to have earned it. The tell is that the failure is *monotonic in the user's own progress*: when a mechanic gets rarer the better the user does, suspect two scales before suspecting a tuning constant.
  - **Job interests (`manager/interests.ts`, `nationalManager/interests.ts`, 2026-09-13, user ask).** The user names up to `MANAGER_MAX_INTERESTS`/`NATIONAL_MAX_INTERESTS` (3) clubs and countries on the Manager page (`JobInterestsPanel`); each rolls once per offer pass at `*_INTEREST_CHANCE` × `interestReach(prestige, target, band)`. Reach is **1 at or below the offer target** (it deliberately bypasses the employed step-up filter — "I want to drop down to that club" is the case the ordinary list can never produce) and fades to 0 at `target + *_OFFER_BAND`, so an interest can never reach a job the ordinary band calls out of reach. **Rolls are on their own streams (972/973), keyed per club/nation, so a save with no interests draws a byte-identical offer list** — pinned by `jobInterests.test.ts`. The target is shared via `clubOfferTarget`/`nationOfferTarget` so the page's "within reach" label and the generator can't disagree. Hits go first and survive the `*_MAX_OFFERS` cap. Optional fields (absent = none), no migration; `switchClub`/`takeNationalJob` drop the taken job from the list. Country picks validate against the latest `powerRankings` snapshot, the only set offers are drawn from, so a season-1 save has none to pick yet.
  - **There is no unemployed state, by design** — every page assumes a user club, so a sacked manager's offer list is guaranteed non-empty (`generateJobOffers` drops the band filter rather than return nothing) and he must accept one. `ManagerState.sackingEnabled` is the user-facing "never sack me" switch (user call, 2026-08-23); offers still arrive so moving clubs stays available.
  - Reputation is **derived** from `ManagerStint[]` (`managerReputation`), never stored, so the formula can be retuned without rewriting careers. The stints themselves store raw results because the board judges at the end of a season *before* it is appended to `seasonHistory` — deriving would need a special case for exactly the moment it matters. Migration backfills **confidence 100** (the board never watched those seasons; an upgrading dynasty must not be one bad year from a sacking) and reconstructs titles/trophies from `seasonHistory`/`cupHistory`/`shieldHistory`/`domesticCupHistory` so an established save arrives with the reputation it earned; `overperformance` stays 0, being unreconstructable without past squad ratings.

**National team management (`core/nationalManager/`, `LeagueStore.nationalManager`, 2026-08-29)**
- **The user can manage a country alongside their club.** Chosen on `/new-league` (a nullable 6th param to `createLeagueState`) or accepted later from a federation's offer. Three pages: `/national-teams/player-pool` (who is in the 23 — a search across every eligible player in the country), `/national-teams/my-squad` (the team sheet — shape and XI, on the club Roster page's own `PitchField`, whose nine club-only props were made optional for it) and `/national-teams/federation` (the portal — confidence, verdict, offers, career). Naming the squad and picking the eleven are split because they are different jobs on different data, and together on one screen the several-hundred-row search drowned a team sheet of 23. Both squad pages mark each player's club with `DivisionBadge` (country flag + D1/D2, extracted from Power Rankings on its second use rather than copied — its two halves have to agree about which competition they describe) through a shared `useClubIndex`/`ClubCell` in `nationalTeams/shared.tsx`, because the two pages list the same players and would read as a bug the moment they described one differently. **The badge earns its room here in a way it would not on a club roster**: a national squad is drawn from the whole world, so without it an English international at an Italian second-division club is indistinguishable from one at a big-four side. The flag is the *league's* country, never the player's. Deliberately shaped as a mirror of `core/manager`, because it is the same relationship from a different chair.
- **`nation: null` is an ordinary, permanent, playable state, and that is the one structural difference from the club career.** A sacked club manager must take another job because every page assumes a club; a manager with no country is simply a save that plays the way every save did before this shipped. So nothing here ever blocks the offseason, `generateNationOffers` may legitimately return an empty list (where `generateJobOffers` drops its filters rather than do so), and `migrate.ts` backfills `emptyNationalManagerState()` with **nothing reconstructed** — those campaigns were played with nobody in charge.
- **You can hold a country that can't field a team yet (2026-09-19, user ask).** New League and God Mode's Switch Country both offer `APPOINTABLE_NATIONS` (every name-pool nation with a confederation), not just the ones clearing `isEligibleNation`; the only gate left on the God Mode action is having a confederation. **It needed no core change**: such a country is simply absent from `buildSquads`' field, so `fieldExpectation` returns null, `reviewNationalCampaign` judges nothing, confidence and the stint sit still, and offers still arrive (its prestige reads 0, so every job is in reach). Once it clears the bar (academy kids count, since the pool is every player of that nationality) it enters the next campaign drawn with the user already in charge. `nationPoolStatus` reports how far off it is and `DormantNationNote` shows that on Player Pool / My Squad / Federation, replacing each page's ordinary "no campaign" copy rather than sitting beside it. The Start-time refusal on New League is gone.
- **The whole match-side mechanism is one field and one line.** `NationSquad.starters` (optional, so no migration) plus `nationMatchData` passing `squad.starters ?? null` instead of the hardcoded `null` it had. Everything below that is the club lineup path unchanged — `leagueMatchData` → `resolveXI` → `isValidStarters` — so a stale national XI degrades to the auto-pick exactly as a club's does.
  - **`resolveXI` is all-or-nothing, and that bites harder here than it does for a club.** `isValidStarters` fails if *any* named pid doesn't resolve, so one unavailable man discards the whole eleven and `selectXI` re-picks all eleven — it does not keep the other ten and fill the gap. A club lineup is re-examined every week; a **qualifying campaign spans three offseasons**, so a national XI genuinely outlives its players and this fires in ordinary play. It is the behaviour we want (never field ten) but it is *not* what "the eleven fills in around the gap" would mean, and the Manual, the changelog entry and the page copy were all written wrong on this once and corrected — say "the game picks that whole eleven for you", not "he's skipped".
- **`editableSquad` (`international/userSquad.ts`) is the single answer to "which squad does an edit apply to".** A campaign's squads live in three places (qualifying, the World Cup, an array of confederation cups) and which one you are picking for follows the **pending stage**, not "the most recent campaign" — editing only means anything while a stage is left to play. `displaySquad` is the read-only fallback so the page has something to show between campaigns. The action layer, the page and the Dashboard nudge all go through them, so they cannot disagree.
- **Expectation is read off `IntlPowerSnapshot`, never off the named squad, and that is the un-gameable half.** `core/manager/expectation.ts` goes to great lengths to build a club's bar out of things a transfer window can't touch, because squad quality is what a club manager controls. **A national manager controls none of it** — he cannot sign, sell, release or develop anyone — so player strength is a safe bar, and the file says so. The one lever he *does* have is naming a weak squad, which is why the rank comes from the snapshot (built by `buildSquads`/`selectSquad` from each nation's **best available** eleven) rather than `squad.rating`. Do not "simplify" that to the stored rating; it reopens the teardown exploit the club side exists to prevent. `fieldExpectation` then ranks within the campaign's own field, since a confederation cup is not the world.
- **Judged per campaign, not per season, and therefore inside `simOffseason` rather than at the `simThrough` boundary where `reviewSeason` sits.** Forced by the calendar: a campaign is *drawn* at the boundary but *played* during the advance, so at the boundary there is nothing to judge. `reviewNationalCampaign` runs immediately after `simThroughInternational`, which also means it sees a finished campaign even when the user skipped every stage. `concludedCampaigns` detects what finished from the campaign objects (`season`, `championNid`, `qualified.length`, and `qualifying.season + INTL_QUAL_LEGS - 1`) rather than from `season % INTL_CYCLE_YEARS`, so a save that joined the cycle late or a world too small for confederation cups can't leave a campaign silently unjudged. In the shipped cadence exactly one concludes per offseason, in three offseasons of four.
- **Qualifying needs its own placement function, and the clamp is the reason.** `tournamentPlacement` bands a finish by the round the nation went out in (champion 1, runner-up 2, beaten semi-finalists 3.5, quarters 6.5, R16 12.5, group exit the midpoint of what's left), derived from the bracket's own depth so a 16-nation confederation cup reads correctly. Qualifying has **no granularity** — you are in the finals or you are not — so an unclamped midpoint reads a favourite's routine qualification as a collapse (expected 3rd, "finished" 16th). `qualifyingPlacement` therefore clamps *toward* expectation, which makes all four cases come out with the right sign: qualifying only helps if you weren't expected to, missing out only hurts if you were.
- **Offers draw on `NATIONAL_OFFER_STREAM` (971), distinct from the club side's 970**, and nothing here touches the shared `rng`. Generated every offseason regardless of whether a campaign concluded, because that is the only route back in for an unemployed manager — hence `NATIONAL_OFFER_UNEMPLOYED_CHANCE` (0.55) against the employed 0.14, and the step-up filter lifting when you hold no job.
- **`NATIONAL_*` is a second constant block on purpose**, not a reuse of `MANAGER_*`: the two jobs are judged on different things at different cadences, and folding them together would let a club-side retune silently change who gets sacked by their country. Same argument as `WORLD_AWARD_OVR_WEIGHT` beside `AWARD_OVR_WEIGHT`. `NATIONAL_CONFIDENCE_SWING` is 46 against the club's 70 because a 32-nation field spreads far wider than a league finish does. `confidenceMood`/`confidenceLabel` *are* shared — both are pure display of a 0-100 number.
- **Sits out a multi-season jump**, guarded on `meta.userTid === AUTOPILOT_TID`. Same reasoning the club board's guard documents (you weren't picking the team), plus it stops a jump ending with a country lost over campaigns the user never saw.
- **`leaveNationalJob` clears `starters` on the departed nation's live squads but deliberately keeps the named 23.** A national team owns nothing — no budget, no contracts, no roster of its own — so unlike `switchClub` there is no handover to unwind; the manual eleven is the only user-only state, and a successor inherits a squad and picks a team from it. `takeNationalJob` is therefore safe at any point in the season, unlike a club switch.
- **Gated by a determinism test** (`nationalManager.test.ts`): one offseason run with and without a managed nation must produce identical `players`/`teams`/`transfers`/`seasonHistory`/`international`. If that ever fails, every dynasty audit in `scripts/` is measuring two different games depending on whether the user had a national job.

**Roster & tactics**
- **Starting XI** — user drag-drops between XI and bench *and* between two XI slots, persisted as `StoredTeam.starters` (11 pids, `null`=auto). `resolveXI` (`core/lineup/resolveXI.ts`) falls back to `selectXI` auto-pick when invalid. Only the user's club sets `starters`; AI always auto-picks. **The swap rule is `core/lineup/swapStarters.ts`, not the page** — `Roster.tsx` used to reject any drag where both ends were on the same side with the comment "nothing to swap", which was true only while composites bucketed by natural position; since they bucket by **slot** (2026-08-12), a starter-to-starter drag is a genuine tactical move, so that guard was lifted (2026-08-14) and only the bench-to-bench case is a no-op. - **Formations** — 16 shapes in `FORMATIONS` (`core/lineup/formations.ts`), `StoredTeam.formation` (default `4-3-3`). A real tactical lever: the match sim rolls up composites from the XI each club's formation fields (`teamFormation`/`teamSlots`). AI clubs auto-pick their strongest shape (`chooseBestFormation`, refreshed end of each transfer window via `assignAIFormations`; never the user's). The user can opt into the same pick via a **"Best XI"** button on Roster (`autoPickBestXIAction` in `LeagueContext`, runs `chooseBestFormation` on the user roster + clears `starters`). `PitchField.tsx` + `pitchLayout.ts` render per-formation layouts; every UI surface derives from `FORMATION_IDS`, so adding a shape needs only the slot array + its layout.
  - **A shape must be a distinct slot multiset, and the obvious additions aren't (2026-08-15, when 7 shapes were added).** Composites bucket by slot and nothing in the sim knows where a slot is *drawn*, so two shapes with the same slots are the same team: **4-1-4-1 is exactly 4-3-3** (GK/CB2/FB2/DM/CM2/W2/ST) and **4-2-4 is exactly 4-4-2** — adding them would only add ties for `chooseBestFormation` to break. A test pins distinctness. The corollary is that back-3 vs back-5 can't be carried by wing-back height (no such axis); it's carried by the midfield band instead — 3-4-3 fields CM2 where 5-2-3 fields DM2, 3-5-2 fields CM2+AM where 5-2-1-2 fields DM2+AM.
  - **Order is load-bearing**: `chooseBestFormation` breaks ties by `FORMATION_IDS` order, so 4-3-3 stays first (it's the default, and the "11-man roster ties to 4-3-3" test depends on it) and new shapes append.
  - **Measured before shipping** (`scripts/formationSpreadProbe.ts`, **4 seeds** × season 3, 319 AI clubs each): the risk is a new shape whose slots happen to match `ROSTER_COMPOSITION` winning everywhere and making every club play alike. It went the other way on every seed — all 16 shapes get used, and the most common shape's share *fell* (25.7→19.4, 20.7→16.6, 20.4→16.0, 18.5→13.5), with 29.8–33.9% of clubs switching shape for +0.145 to +0.203 raw ovr per starter. **The baseline itself moves ~7 points across seeds, so quote the range, not one draw** — seed 1 happens to be the highest of the four, and an earlier version of this entry generalised from it alone. The probe also reports a **slot-adjusted** figure because `chooseBestFormation` ranks on **raw `p.ovr`, not `ovrAtSlot`** (pre-existing), so a shape can in principle win the pick by fielding a strong man in a slot foreign to him and then play worse; measured, the two track on all four seeds (adjusted +0.134 to +0.193, never more than 0.011 below raw) and 1.9–2.8% of clubs end up marginally worse than their legacy pick. Re-run the probe before adding more shapes — a gap opening between those two numbers is the signal that the raw-ovr ranking needs to become slot-aware.
  - **Dynasty-audited** (`scripts/slotChurnAudit.ts`, 15 seasons × **4 seeds**, run on both this branch and its merge-base — the script is byte-identical on both sides). The risk was the same one the slot-aware composites change was audited for: a shape lever that sharpens the strong/weak gap into a procession. It doesn't. Title churn is **flat-to-better and straddles zero per seed** (distinct champions per league 8.13 → 8.54 on the mean, per seed +1.37/0.00/−0.62/+0.88; dominant club's title share 31.5% → 28.8%, per seed −6.7/+0.8/+1.7/−6.7) — so "no procession" is what the data supports, **not** "title races opened up". Anti-inflation holds (mean ovr drift over 15 seasons 0.00 → 0.15, against the ~+4 over 25 seasons main already carries for unrelated reasons; two seeds sit at +0.3-0.4 where the baseline was ~0, so it is small rather than literally zero). D2 ceiling unchanged (45 → 44 over-threshold AI player-seasons across the four seeds).
  - **The one systematic effect is champion points, down on ALL FOUR seeds** (77.82 → 77.34, per seed −0.65/−0.53/−0.29/−0.43). That direction is the mechanism working as designed: every club gets a marginally better XI, not just the strong ones, so the champion's margin over the field narrows. It is worth knowing because the **M1 standings gate (`simThrough.test.ts`, champion 78-94 pts) already sits near its floor by construction** and is noisy over its 5-season sample — the gate passes here, but roughly half a point of its headroom is now spent. **Re-measure that gate over ≥15 seasons, not its own sample, before adding more shapes**; a further shape batch pushing champion points down again is the thing that would fail it.

Optional field, no migration, zero rng draws, user club only. Releasing or promoting on the spot still works as before, and a user who never opens the Academy still has a working pipeline — the property the trial group lacked (an ignored trial screen emptied the academy, which is what forced `ensureUserRosterSafety`'s market fallback below).

Without it an 18+ prospect signed out of free agency on an ordinary deal was kept off expiry and promoted onto a senior wage at 5.0, and hid the page's final-year banner (code review, 2026-09-13). Offseason step 1.5 puts those kids in `releaseExpiredContracts`' skip set; step 5.0 `resolveAcademyCheckpoints` promotes graduates while the senior roster is under `ROSTER_CAP` (best-scouted first), releases the rest, and re-contracts 16-year-olds to 18; step 5.4 `trimAcademyToCap` (`ACADEMY_ROSTER_CAP` 20) cuts the lowest-scouted 16-year-olds once the intake has arrived and the real headcount is known. **Only that year is ever cut** — a kid on his first deal isn't at a decision and one past 16 was kept at it — so the academy may legitimately sit over the cap. `renewalsDue("academy")` skips checkpoint kids and the Academy page hides their Extend button, since re-signing cannot move a deal that already runs to the cut.
  - **Ranked on the MIDDLE OF THE SCOUTING BAND as of the season being viewed, never on true potential.** Ranking on the truth would make the defaults quietly perfect and hand the hidden number back through who survived. Counting `team.roster` minus expiring deals had borrowed players staying and returning ones missing. `academyPipeline.test.ts` pins it against the real `processLoanReturns`/`releaseExpiredContracts`.
    - **What can't be known is assumed cautiously, so `promote`/`keep` are promises and `release`/`atRisk` are worst cases**: intake is taken at the max, and summer retirements (a roll) and a borrowed player's parent renewing him are assumed not to free places.
  - **Kids GROW before `YOUTH_BASE_REFERENCE_AGE` (16), toward ratings rolled up front, and land on them exactly (`Player.youthTarget`, `youthRatingsAt` in `players/progression.ts`, 2026-09-13, user ask).** `generatePlayer` has no age term on the youth path, so a kid rolled at 14 IS a 16-year-old's ratings. `enrolAcademyYouth` stores those as `youthTarget` and shows him `youthShortfall` below them: one season of the model's own growth at 16 (`youthGrowthPerSeason`, both rating groups at full academy minutes) per year short, the final year split early/late per kid by a pid hash (`ACADEMY_GROWTH_TIMING_SPREAD`). `progressPlayer` still discards the rating step at the RETURN (every draw spent, as `ratingsLocked` does), sets him to `youthRatingsAt(target, age + 1)`, and drops the field the season he lands; `estimatePotential` still forecasts from 16, off the target, so potential never moves on the way.
    - **Landing on a pre-rolled number IS the design, not a shortcut (user call).** Starting kids lower and letting them grow freely is right only on average, and it is exactly what the world-wide age-15 attempt measured failing: the soft floor and `RATING_MIN` clamp a lowered start away at weak academies. Here the sixteen-year-old average AND spread are identical to generating at 16 by construction, and no draw count moves, so world containment is untouched.
    - **The shortfall is UNIFORM across every rating.** A uniform shift cancels out of every position's OVR comparison, so a kid's best position is the same at 14 as at 16 and `changedPosition`'s window never reads a growth artifact. Only the `RATING_MIN` clamp bends it.
    - God Mode rating edits move the target by the same delta (`shiftTarget`), so an edited kid keeps growing. A kid enrolled before this carries no target and holds still until 16, the old behaviour, so nothing to migrate.
  - **World containment rests on three things, all carried over from the trial group:** (a) the extras are generated after the world loop, so every other club keeps its pids (`developmentBias`/`isGenerational` hash off them); (b) the extras draw on `YOUTH_TRIAL_STREAM` (88, value kept so saves draw the same kids); (c) the user's ordinary intake stays inside the loop on the shared rng and generating it at 14 instead of 16 spends **identical** draws, because `estimatePotential` forecasts from 16 either way — only `born` differs. `scoutDirections.test.ts` fingerprints the rest of the world; `youthIntake.test.ts` pins (a) and the pre-16 lock through real offseasons.

Uncorrected it is +5 OVR everywhere and weak-league insolvency; three generation-side corrections failed because `softFloorBase` and `RATING_MIN` clamp a lower starting level away (~0.27 OVR per `YOUTH_BASE_OFFSET` point); the growth-side discard held the big four (68.12 against 68.47 at season 21, 4 seeds) but **deflated every other country 0.9-3.0 and widened BIG4->Serbia +8.39 -> +11.04**, mechanism unmeasured. Small, unmeasured, stated.   - **Scout directions (`StoredTeam.scoutingRegions`/`scoutingPositions`, `scouting/scoutDirections.ts`, 2026-09-01).** Two things the user tells his youth scouts: where to look, and which positions to look for. **They do not reach equally far, and the asymmetry is forced rather than chosen** — a country is a post-hoc relabel of the whole yearly intake, while a position decides how a player is *generated* and so can only touch the extras. Set on one panel, saved by one action (`setScoutDirectionsAction`), read at one point in the offseason, and sanitized through `scoutDirectionsOf` on both the way in and the way out.

Re-drawing afterwards on `YOUTH_TRIAL_STREAM` cannot. It covers the whole group, ordinary intake included, because the setting should describe everyone the scouts turned up.

The user's *ordinary* intake is generated inside the world loop on the **shared** `rng`, so re-weighting that draw would shift the stream by four and re-roll every club generated after his. The extras are drawn on the trial stream, where it is free. **The share is 0.75 against the country blend's 0.6 precisely BECAUSE it reaches less of the group** — extras are ~5-9 of a 10-12 group, so a higher within-extras share lands the skew the user actually sees in the same place a country's does. Still a skew and not a filter, for the reason the country blend is one. `targetedPositionCdf` keeps it to exactly one draw, same as the plain `ROSTER_COMPOSITION` table.

**Jump forward N seasons, club on autopilot** (`core/autopilot.ts`, `LeagueStore.aiManagedSeasons`, 2026-08-19)

A jumped world does differ from before, since the club's youth no longer passes through `trimRosterSurplus`, which is the point.

**Spectator saves — a league nobody manages (`core/spectator.ts`, `SPECTATOR_TID` -3, 2026-08-31)**
- **It is the jump's own trick made permanent, and that is why it needed no sim code.** `meta.userTid` points at a tid no club owns, so the ~110 "every club except `userTid`" gates fall through onto the whole world. `simThrough`/`simOffseason` gained **no branch**, no rng draw moved, and the shipped gate is that a spectated season is **bit-identical to an autopiloted one** (`test/core/spectator.test.ts` compares `players`/`teams`/`transfers`/`seasonHistory`/`activeLoans`/`international` after a season plus an offseason). If that ever fails, something has started branching on *which* club-less tid it is looking at.
- **The invariant two files spell out is qualified, not repealed, and the wording matters.** `autopilot.ts` says its sentinel "can never reach disk"; `manager/types.ts` says "there is no unemployed state, by design". Both still hold *within a managed save* — a sacked manager must still take one of the offers, and nothing in a career can strand him. What is now true is narrower: **within one save, whether anybody manages a club never changes — outside God Mode.** A spectator save is club-free from creation and has no route in or out through ordinary play, which is precisely what makes it safe — every handover problem `switchClub` exists to solve (an academy of zombie players an AI club will never promote, pay off or release; a stale manual XI; live negotiations with nobody on one end) arises only when a club *changes hands*, and here none ever does. Both doc comments were updated to say so; don't "fix" them back.
- **Its own sentinel rather than reusing `AUTOPILOT_TID`, deliberately.** The two states differ: autopilot is temporary and reversible by construction (`endAutopilot` restores a real tid and re-fogs the squad, repairs that only mean anything because someone is taking the club back), a spectator has nothing to restore. Sharing one number would make `isSpectator` unanswerable and would let the next jump-specific `if (userTid === AUTOPILOT_TID)` silently catch spectator saves. A spectator can still **jump** — `beginAutopilot` swaps -3 for -2 and `endAutopilot` swaps it back, both no-ops in substance — and that needed no code.
- **The only genuine functional hole was advancing time**, since `Dashboard` early-returns on a missing `userTeam` and `/set-scouting` (which owns the offseason advance) bounces without one. `SpectatorDashboard.tsx` is its own page rather than conditionals through `Dashboard`, which is built end to end around a club: table position, next fixture, injuries, board meter, wage bill, scouting slider, squad leaders. `useOffseasonAdvance()` is the **single** decision of which advance route a save takes (checkpoint vs straight through), shared by the Dashboard card and the TopBar dropdown so the two can't disagree.
- **Nothing crashes without the guard, which is why the guard is about copy rather than safety.** Every club page already early-returns "Team not found." or renders empty. `ClubOnly` wraps those routes in `App.tsx` — one place, so a page added to that section later can't forget it — purely so a typed URL reads as the game working. **The Watchlist is deliberately outside it** and moves up into the sidebar's League section: it is a note about players anywhere in the world, not an instruction to a club, the same reason `switchClub` keeps it across a move.
- **A spectator holds no jobs at all**, so `createLeagueState` forces `userNation` null (against every caller, not just the one screen that offers it) and `reviewNationalCampaign` early-returns — without that its offer pass still runs, since it deliberately doesn't depend on *holding* a job, and would hand a spectator a country. `emptyManagerState` opens **no stint**, mirroring `emptyNationalManagerState` for a declined country; that one absence is what keeps the whole career machinery quiet with no extra branch (`currentStint` undefined → `reviewSeason` takes the early out it already had).
- **The news feed degrades to its world tier by construction, not by a special case.** `buildSeasonTimeline` keys its middle tier off `userCompId`, and a spectator has no league of their own, so passing `undefined` leaves exactly the world tier — which is the right reading. `newsHeadlineNode` was **extracted** from `Dashboard.tsx` (not copied) into `components/NewsHeadline.tsx` so the two dashboards can't describe the same event differently; the News Feed page's separate, richer renderer predates this and is left alone.
- **God Mode's Switch Club is the one deliberate exception, and it needed no code.** A spectator taking a club there is a one-way trip into a managed save, which is right for a sandbox that is guardrail-free by definition and is the only mercy for someone twenty seasons into a choice they regret. It is safe for free because `switchClub` already guards its handover on *finding* a departing club (`if (old)`), so with none there is nothing to dissolve or hand back, and `stints` being empty means the new stint is simply the first. Only that direction exists — nothing turns a managed save into a spectator one.
- **Schedule is club-only despite living under League in the sidebar** — the page is your club's fixture list end to end, so without a club it is an empty table rather than a world schedule. Hidden and wrapped like the Team pages. A real world fixture browser would be its own feature.
- **The dashboard's world panels live in `ui/pages/worldPanels.tsx` and read nothing user-specific**, which is why **both** dashboards carry them off one definition — the managed one appends them below everything about your club (nothing existing moves) and passes `highlightTid` so the power board picks you out; the spectator one has no club to pick out and passes none. Power rankings shows the **power score, not OVR** — the board is ordered by power (strength blended with form), and an OVR column beside it runs 76, 75, 74, 77 and reads as an unsorted list; the full page gets away with both because it has a header and a help note. `MiniBracket` is a **second drawing** of a bracket, not a shrunk one: `/cup`'s is a CSS-grid tree (`gridRow: span 2**round` with connector styling keyed to slot percentages) and does not survive being squeezed into a card. It shows **played ties only** — the big bracket emits "to be decided" placeholders for every unreached round, which is right on a page about the competition and wrong in a panel that would then sit three-quarters empty from August to March. It takes rendered nodes for the two sides, which is the whole reason one component serves all four brackets (two club competitions, two international).
- **A synthetic `CupState` needs a `leaguePhase`, and this bit the panel tests exactly as CLAUDE.md's `liveCandidates` note warns.** `isSwissCup` is just `!!cup.leaguePhase`, so a fixture without one is a **legacy** cup: `koRoundsOf` returns a fixed 4 and an eight-club field's first round comes out labelled "Round of 16" instead of the quarter-finals. Every cup a real save builds is Swiss.
- **`MatchdayPanel` shows the last round PLAYED, not the next one due**, and it replaced the spectator dashboard's news panel. Results are the payoff of the sim button above it: showing the next fixtures instead leaves the scorelines nowhere on the page, and a column of "v" adds little to the table beside it. It falls back to the fixtures only before a ball is kicked, and an offseason still shows the season's last round because `played` is emptied when the new season *starts*, not when the old one ends. It takes a `compId` rather than owning a picker, so the Table's dropdown governs the whole row — and it prints the competition's name so that pairing can't read as a coincidence. **Full club names here, abbreviations in the brackets**: a bracket is a wide grid of short cells where the shape carries the meaning; this is ten lines with room to say who played.
- **The sim overlay's ticker counts the matchday for a spectator; showing one world match was tried and rejected (user call).** The strip is built entirely out of *your* results, so a club-less save got a placeholder for the length of a season sim. The first fix picked a single world game by widest winning margin — and whatever rule picks it, the answer is a match between two clubs the viewer has no stake in, which is noise however it is chosen. It now shows games-and-goals for the whole round: never arbitrary, moves every matchday, a sum rather than a choice. Ranking ~200 matches a matchday by how interesting they are is a real feature and not one to guess at inside an animation.
- **The international panel keys off the four-season cycle, not off a display rule** — `isTournamentSeason` (`season % 4 === 0`, the World Cup) and `isConfederationCupSeason` (`season % 4 === 2`), so it appears every other offseason and the two qualifying-only years correctly render nothing. Bracket depth comes from `bracket.length`, **not** from how many rounds have been played: naming a round counts backwards from the final, so deriving depth from played rounds calls a half-finished tournament's first round its final (the existing `ConfederationCups.tsx` derives it the played-rounds way and degrades for exactly this reason).
- **No persisted field and no migration** — the mode *is* `meta.userTid`, which every save already carries, and no existing save can become one. Export/import needs nothing (it only checks the tid is a number). **`league_created`'s `tier` went `1 | 2` → `1 | 2 | null`** rather than defaulting, because `tierForTid` answers 1 for a tid it cannot find and a default would file every spectator save as a top-flight start.


**God Mode** (`core/godMode.ts`) — per-save sandbox (`LeagueStore.godMode`). Guardrail-free editing (bypasses fees/budget/cap/depth-floor): move/release/edit/create players, set club finances; un-fogs potential everywhere. `detachPlayer` scrubs a pid from all rosters + transient state. `PlayerEditModal.tsx`, `/god-mode` hub. Scope-outs: no add/delete clubs, no hard-delete (release instead), no forcing match results.
- **Take charge of any club (`godModeSwitchClubAction`, God Mode's Switch Club tab, 2026-08-29).** Reuses `switchClub` rather than reassigning `meta.userTid` itself, and that is the whole point: several `StoredTeam` fields are user-only by convention (the academy above all — see the Manager career entry for why an AI club that inherits one populated is a state nothing unwinds), so a second reassignment path would be a second chance to get the handover wrong. All it removes are the two gates that make an offer an offer — that a club asked, and that it is the offseason. **The offseason gate is what forced the one core change**: `switchClub` opened the new stint at `season + 1` unconditionally, which is right at a boundary and wrong mid-season, where it would date the stint a year forward and leave the career table claiming the user was nowhere while they were picking that squad. It now takes an optional `startSeason` (default `season + 1`, so the job-offer path is byte-identical) and the God Mode action passes `l.season` outside the offseason. Pure and rng-free like `switchClub` itself, no persisted field, no migration.
- **Take charge of any country (`godModeTakeNationalJobAction`, God Mode's Switch Country tab, 2026-09-02).** The national twin of the above, and it needed **no core change at all** — `takeNationalJob` was already unconditional, already good at any point in the season and already closes the old stint and hands that nation's eleven back, because a national team owns nothing a mid-season handover could strand (`switchNation.ts` says so at length). So the action is the offer gate removed and two others kept. **(a)** The country must clear `isEligibleNation` against the live pool, checked with the identical call the picker builds its list from — the new `manageableNations(players)` (`international/squads.ts`), which is the *same* rule `buildSquads` qualifies a campaign's field by, so the picker cannot offer a country the gate then refuses (a test pins the two lists equal). It is computed live rather than read off `international.powerRankings`, because a season-1 save has no snapshot yet and that is exactly the save someone wants this in. **(b) A spectator save is refused**, unlike Switch Club which lets a spectator take a club as a deliberate one-way trip. The asymmetry is the point: taking a club *ends* spectating and every system then works, whereas `reviewNationalCampaign` early-returns for a spectator, so a country taken there would never be judged, never sack you and never lead to another offer — the whole federation career would be silently inert rather than merely unusual. The tab says so and points at Switch Club. Stepping down reuses the existing `leaveNationalJobAction` rather than a God Mode variant (nothing about leaving is gated in the first place).
- **Freeze a player's development (`Player.ratingsLocked`, 2026-09-07).** A locked player's ratings, ovr, listed position and potential stop moving every offseason until it is cleared. It closes the gap that made the player editor half a tool: you could set a veteran to anything, and the age curve took it back the next summer, so the only way to hold a player still was to re-edit him annually.
  - **The branch sits at the RETURN of `progressPlayer`, not at its top, and that placement is the whole safety argument.** `stepRatings` and `estimatePotential` are both drawn from the **shared** `rng`, so an early exit would spend fewer draws and shift the stream for every player progressed after him — one locked player silently re-rolling the rest of the world, the failure this file documents over and over. The draws are therefore spent exactly as they would have been and only the *result* is discarded. `progression.test.ts` pins the draw count equal with and without the lock, which is the test that fails if anyone "optimizes" the wasted work away.
  - **A locked player still gets his `hist` snapshot appended**, carrying the frozen values. That array is what the OVR/value charts plot and what `awards.ovrDuringSeason` reads, so skipping it leaves a hole in his career rather than a flat line through it — and `ovrDuringSeason` would then read a stale season for every award scored after the lock.
  - **Potential is frozen with the rest, deliberately.** Re-estimating it off frozen ratings as he ages is defensible (it is a scout's read, and it would decay), but it would quietly overwrite the number God Mode was just used to set, and "locked" reading as "locked except this one field" is the worse surprise. Freezing it is also what makes `peakOvr` safe: `ovr` never moves, so the running maximum is inert unless an edit raised him, which the existing `beatsPeak` branch still picks up.
  - **The lock is not a write guard.** `applyPlayerEdit` still applies to a locked player — it stops the *simulation*, not the sandbox — and nothing else about him changes (contracts, injuries, transfers, retirement, the market's valuation of him all carry on).
  - **Optional field, absent = unlocked, and `applyPlayerEdit` normalizes to true-or-absent rather than storing `false`.** That is what makes "absent means unlocked" literally true of every player rather than merely of the ones nobody has edited, and it is why `migrate.ts` needs no backfill (`migratePlayer` spreads `...p`, and pre-feature saves genuinely had no locked players — exact, not a guess). `PlayerEdit.ratingsLocked` is likewise tri-state: omitted leaves the setting alone, so the one-click toggles can send that field alone without clearing anything else.
  - **Surfaced in three places on purpose, and the badge is deliberately NOT gated on `league.godMode`.** God Mode is a switch the user can turn back off while the lock persists, so hiding the marker would leave a player who has mysteriously stopped developing with nothing on screen explaining why. Profile header marker + one-click button, edit-modal checkbox (staged with the other two clear-this boxes, since it saves with the edit — see the `form-check`/`form-switch` rule under UI conventions), and a Lock button + `locked` marker per row in the God Mode Roster Builder, which is the only surface that lists a squad and so the only one where freezing eleven players is bearable.
- **Edit the award formulas (`core/awardFormula.ts`, `LeagueStore.awardFormula`, God Mode's Awards tab, 2026-09-14, user ask).** Every weight `potyScore`/`totsScore`/`computeWorldAwards` read is one `AwardFormula` value; `DEFAULT_AWARD_FORMULA` is the constants gathered up and frozen, and each scorer takes the formula as a trailing param defaulting to it. **The default is bit-identical by construction** (`ratingWeight` 1 multiplies exactly, every other weight is the same number in the same expression), pinned by `awardFormula.test.ts` and by every existing award test passing unmodified. Optional field, absent = shipped, **no migration** (`migrateFields` spreads `...league`). Four things worth knowing.
  - **Always read it through `resolveAwardFormula`**, which merges the stored edit over the defaults field by field and replaces any non-finite number with the default. A cleared input is NaN, and NaN in a score silently breaks every ranking (`>` against NaN is false). The action also stores a formula equal to the default as no field at all, so "absent means shipped" stays literally true and the Awards page's custom note can key off it.
  - **Baselines are deliberately NOT editable** (`AWARD_OVR_BASELINE`, `TOTS_KEEPER_SAVE_PCT_BASELINE`): they subtract one constant from every player an award compares, so they cannot change a winner. Nor are the pro-rating shapes (full-season appearances, the trophy-strength floor and cap).
  - **Read once per offseason (step 3) and never retroactively**, same as the awards themselves. Both passes get the same resolved value, so league XIs and world awards can't be scored on two formulas in one season.
  - **It moves the world, not just the cabinet.** A Team of the Season place feeds `isProtectedStar`, which the AI market reads before a jitter draw, so an edited TOTS formula changes the draw count and every season after. Fine for a sandbox; it is why this lives in God Mode and why the copy says so. Stays in force with God Mode off, like `ratingsLocked`.
  - **The first cut was forty bare decimals and read as unusable in a browser (user, same day); the tab is now built so nobody has to reason about raw weights.** Three pieces: `AWARD_PRESETS` (the shipped formula with one idea turned up; `matchingPreset` recognises a saved one so it doesn't reopen as "custom"), a **preview** (`core/awardPreview.ts`, `scoreSeasonAwards`) that re-scores the last finished season under the draft, and every weight explained as what it is worth in forward goals, behind a *Fine-tune* disclosure. **The preview compares the draft against `scoreSeasonAwards` on the save's CURRENT formula, never against the winners stored on the season**: retirees are gone from the pool, so the stored winners can differ for reasons that have nothing to do with the formula, and comparing against them would report changes the edit didn't make. A test pins that the preview scores a season exactly as the offseason's two passes do. The standalone reset button went with it: *As shipped* is a preset.
