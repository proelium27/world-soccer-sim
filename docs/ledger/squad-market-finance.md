**Squad development**
- **Youth Academy** (user's club only) — `StoredTeam.academyRoster`, flat stipend wages (`academyContractTerms`). Actions in `core/freeAgency.ts` (`signToAcademy`/`promoteFromAcademy`/`releaseAcademyPlayer`). `ensureUserRosterSafety` auto-promotes to `ROSTER_SAFETY_FLOOR` (18) each offseason so the user's unmanaged roster can't drop below 11 and crash the engine. `/academy`, `/free-agents` (every unsigned player). AI youth still goes straight to roster. The academy fills itself — see "The user's academy is a PIPELINE" under Roster & tactics.
- **Dynamic academy attraction** — recent within-division finishing rank shifts youth-intake quality (`core/players/academyForm.ts`). **Zero-sum by construction** (one club's + is another's −), so it can't reopen the inflation ratchet.
- **Youth base has a soft floor — the flat offset underflowed at weak clubs (`youthGenerationBase`, `players/youth.ts`, 2026-08-17).** `academyBase - YOUTH_BASE_OFFSET` is a *flat* subtraction (34) against an `academyBase` spanning **18.8–63.0**, so it went **negative for 73 of 320 clubs** (as low as −15.2). Every rating then generated below zero and clamped to `RATING_MIN` (1), which yields not a weak player but a **destroyed** one — an ST with speed/passing/dribbling/positioning all 1, plus survivors only where the `star`/`H` tiers or the base-independent `ABS` tier landed above the floor. Measured, one offseason on a fresh world: **472 of 1268 new youth (37%) at OVR 0–15, 98% of them released to free agency the same offseason** (`trimRosterSurplus` ranks by ovr, so they are always the worst at their position). Surfaced by a user save with a 16-year-old ST at **OVR 9**. Fix is a **softplus soft floor**, and the shape is load-bearing: **(a)** `YOUTH_BASE_OFFSET` is a swept anti-inflation constant (40-season mean flat — see its comment), so healthy clubs must be untouched: at the shipped FLOOR=1/SOFTNESS=1 the lift at raw base 6.3 and 11.3 is **0.00** and only 110 clubs move at all. **This is the trap:** a first attempt at FLOOR=4/SOFTNESS=5 looked smooth but lifted **295 of 320 clubs by a mean of +3.67 points** — only 73 are broken, but 275 sit below raw base 20 — which raised end-of-dynasty OVR 1.3-3.1 in *every* league (big four included, via their own tier 2 and the upward drain) and took the solvency audit from 1 failing seed to 4. Anchor "healthy" at raw base < 0, not at some comfortable-looking number. **(b)** A *hard* `Math.max` floor makes every club below the cut generate **identical** youth, erasing the academy gradient exactly where the country ladder is most fragile. **(c)** A proportional offset changes every club and would require redoing the sweep. **(d)** The intended senior→youth gap is ~33 OVR (measured 31.8–33.8 across the healthy range) and that gap is simply **unavailable** to a club whose seniors average 29.4 — the scale bottoms out — so it *must* compress at the bottom; softplus does that smoothly and stays strictly monotonic. Result: the weakest club in the world goes from youth OVR min/mean/max **1/4.1/9 → 5/13.5/25** vs its seniors averaging 29.4, and the strongest club is **bit-identical** (base 29.00, 30/40.1/52). Audited (`scripts/weakLeaguesAudit.ts`, 20 seasons x 4 seeds, run per-seed): ladder OK every seed, mean rung gates pass, and **4/4 seeds solvent where main is 3/4** (main deficits on seed 2 at -£2.0M). **Accepted cost:** clubs between `academyBase` ~18.8 and ~25.5 now generate near-identical youth, so `ACADEMY_FORM_SWING` (added to `academyBase` *before* the transform) has less bite down there. **No rng draw** — pure arithmetic on `academyBase`, so stream *order* is untouched; generated values move, so multi-season baselines shift (single-season `touchStats` is unaffected — youth intake is offseason-only). Separately known and NOT fixed here: `trimRosterSurplus` is ovr-only, so **112 youth with POT ≥ 70 are released every offseason**; making it potential-aware is a deliberate follow-up, and it **must not land before this fix** or weak clubs retain OVR-2 players on the senior roster instead of flushing them.
- **Youth intake position mix** — `generateYouthIntake` draws each prospect's position weighted by `ROSTER_COMPOSITION` (not uniform), so the pipeline matches roster demand (CB/FB/CM 4 slots each get more prospects than DM/AM's 2). Single rng() draw via a precomputed CDF, so RNG-stream *count* is unchanged (only the mapped position moves) — but generation *values* shift, so this is not byte-identical to pre-weighting saves; `generateWorld` is untouched (it never runs youth intake). Note the low-slot positions (DM/AM, 2 each) still structurally leak their *good* surplus into free agency — a club can only house 2, so a prime 3rd gets trimmed — which is why the `/free-agents` list caps per-position on its unfiltered view (see Free agency).
- **Team Rating** — `core/teams/teamRating.ts` (`computeTeamRating`), XI (equal weight) + geometrically-decayed bench, not a flat roster mean. OVR + POT. Shown on Standings (current only) and user's Roster header.

**Free agency**
- **FA transfer hold** (`core/freeAgency.ts`, `faTransferLocked`) — `signFreeAgent` stamps `Player.faSignedSeason` = the season the signing takes effect (offseason signing → `season+1`, mid-season → `season`). While `league.season <= faSignedSeason` the user can't sell him: `inboundOfferCandidates` skips him, and Roster shows "Can't sell yet (just signed)" instead of List-for-Transfer. Kills the buy-a-free-agent-and-flip-him exploit. Optional field, absent on old saves (= unlocked); preserved through progression/`executeTransfer`.
- **Free moves are recorded as transfers** (`FREE_AGENT_TID` = **-1**, `core/transfers/negotiation.ts`) — a free-agent *arrival* is logged as a fee-0 `CompletedTransfer` whose `fromTid` is this sentinel (not a real tid). Load-bearing because club-by-season history is **reconstructed from `league.transfers` alone** (`teamForSeason` in `PlayerProfile.tsx`; also the OVR chart's club colors) — before this, ~19% of all rostered players displayed the *wrong current club*, since an unrecorded free arrival left them attributed to whatever club their last **paid** move landed at. Written by `signFreeAgentAction`, `signToAcademyAction` (academy = a free arrival at the club; promotion academy→senior needs none, same club) and `runAIFreeAgency` via `offseason.ts` step 4. Gotchas: **(a)** departures into free agency are deliberately *not* recorded (would double the log for no display gain — every surface asks "which club in season N", which a release doesn't change); the one lying case, sign-then-cut in the same offseason, is filtered at offseason step 6.47. **(b)** `teamForSeason` must never seed pre-history from the sentinel — a player can sit on a roster for years with no record at all, so seasons before his first record fall back to his present club. **(c)** AI free churn is ~1-2k signings/season, so it's kept out of aggregate feeds (News Feed, per-window transfer lists, Season Preview top transfers) — the user's own free signings still show. **(d)** Any UI rendering a raw from/to tid must go through `clubDisplayName` (`ui/format.ts`) or the sentinel renders as "Unknown"/"Team -1". **(e)** No shared-`rng` draw, so sim results stay bit-identical; no migration (old saves just keep their pre-fix history).
- **AI FA quality poaching** (`runAIFreeAgency`, second pass) — after filling positional shortfalls, each AI club (worst-first) poaches the single best remaining FA that upgrades a position it's already at target depth in (→ composition+1); `trimRosterSurplus` later keeps the best and releases the now-weakest, so the strongest FAs drain out of the pool before the user shops it. Contract length drawn from a **separate seeded stream** (not shared `rng`) → youth generation stays bit-identical.
- **That pass could not do the thing its own comment claims, because it ran BEFORE the step that stocks the pool — a step-ORDER bug, fixed 2026-09-09 (offseason step 6.05).** AI free agency is step 4; `trimRosterSurplus` (step 6) is what puts most of a year's free agents in the pool, and it releases whoever a club is **deepest** at rather than whoever is worst, so what it dumps is routinely better than the clubs shopping for it. Step 4 therefore only ever picked over *last* year's leftovers, the ones every club had already passed on. **Measured over six simmed seasons: the pool at the end of a season is byte-identical to the previous summer's — between one summer and the next, nothing in the world signs a free agent.** The user had a twelve-month monopoly on ~15-25 players a year at ovr 70+, topping out in the mid-80s. That is how the reported save (a promoted club, top-five squad rating, 16th and relegated) assembled an 80-rated squad almost entirely on free transfers. The offseason now runs the pass **again after the trim**, then trims again — the poach deliberately overshoots to composition+1 and relies on a following trim to return the now-weakest, which is what makes it a SWAP rather than squad growth. Placed before the market (6.4) for the same reason the first trim is: nothing bought with a fee should be trimmable in the offseason it was paid for.
  - **On its own seeded stream (`MOP_UP_FA_STREAM`), never `rng`.** The poach and prospect passes already seed contract length per signing, but the shortfall pass uses whatever rng it is handed — and while it signs nobody in practice (a trim removes surplus and cannot create a shortfall), relying on that would be the fragile version. A dedicated stream makes shared-stream order untouched *by construction*.
  - **Dynasty-audited (`weakLeaguesAudit`, 20 seasons × 4 seeds): every seed reads `ladder OK`, every seed is SOLVENT, and every rung passes on the pooled mean.** Pooled on the shipped tree: BIG4→France **+4.21**, France→Netherlands +1.67, Netherlands→Portugal +2.82, Portugal→Belgium −0.46, Belgium→Turkey +1.38, Turkey→Greece +0.18, Greece→Scotland −0.71, Scotland→Serbia +0.92, with block spreads BIG4→Serbia **+10.00** (gate ≥4) and France→Serbia **+5.80** (gate ≥1). Every seed is solvent (minima £0.0-0.1M), against a base that has been red on this gate as recently as #309/#340. **Read the per-seed "FAILURES" as the documented misuse, not as findings** — and note this run is the sharper illustration of why: seeds 1 AND 2 both print `Portugal→Belgium INVERTED` (−1.37, −1.69), the *same* rung twice, which looked systematic until seeds 3 and 4 came back at **+1.37** and **−0.15** and the pooled figure landed at **−0.46**. Two agreeing seeds are still two seeds; Portugal→Belgium is the pair this file already names as the coin-flip. Caveat stated plainly: the merge base was **not** re-measured for this change, which is tolerable only because the treatment passes every gate outright — there is no failure needing attribution. Re-measure both sides before reading any of these numbers as an improvement.
  - **`MOP_UP_MIN_OVR` (80) is the only lever that tempers it, and TWO others were built, measured and deleted first.** Unbounded, the mop-up does not thin the pool, it **erases** it: measured, every band above ovr 70 empty in all six seasons, best signable 55-65. A quality **margin** over the club's weakest at the position does nothing — for a 70-rated free agent to go unsigned, all 626 clubs must fail the bar, and a third-division squad's weakest man sits in the 30s, so somebody always qualifies (margins 0/6/12/18/25 → best remaining free agent 27/28/28/28/28). Capping the **share of clubs** that take part fails on the same arithmetic (12% participation is still 75 clubs against ~25 players). A quality **floor** works because it changes *what is contested* rather than *who contests it*, and it is the realistic reading: a free 85 is worth reopening recruitment for in August, a squad player is not. Shipped result: **80-84 empty, an occasional 85 surviving, 75-79 holding 9-13 and 70-74 holding 9-14 each summer.** Sweep with `scripts/freeAgentWindowProbe.ts`.
- **A free transfer skipped the player-will module entirely, and that was the one route around it (`refusesFreeAgentSigning`, 2026-09-09).** `playerWill.ts`'s own header calls that module the fix for "the single worst realism bug in the game", and `refusesMoveToClub`'s comment says exempting the user "would hand them the very exploit this module exists to close" — yet `signFreeAgent` called neither, so a player who would flatly refuse to be *bought* by a club would happily *sign* for it. Now gated, and so is **`signToAcademy`**, which has an age cap and **no rating cap** and was therefore the same exploit through a cheaper door (a flat stipend instead of an ovr-cubic wage).
  - **`from` is the HIGHER of his last club's stature and `abilityStature(ovr)`, and each half alone leaks.** The last club is recoverable because `SeasonStats.tid` is the only place a *departure* into free agency is written down (`league.transfers` records free arrivals, not releases) — and it is recoverable for **100% of free agents at ovr ≥ 70**, since they played. But it is not sufficient: measured on the reported save, the **85-rated signing had last played for a club of stature 0.158**, so joining a second-division side read as a step *up* and nothing gated it. Good players sit at small clubs constantly, which is exactly what a pool stocked by depth-trimming produces. Ability alone would miss the modest player who spent a career at a giant.
  - **`abilityStature` must use an INDIVIDUAL's band, not a squad's.** `clubStature` normalizes a club's top-16 *mean* against `STATURE_STRENGTH_LO..HI`; running one player's rating through that same band overstates him (a squad averaging 89 contains players well above 89) and put an ovr-95 free agent at a flat 1.0 — **unsignable by any club in the world**. It runs `PLAYER_WILL_CARE_FLOOR..STATURE_STRENGTH_HI` instead, both **by reference**, so a retune of either end moves it structurally.
  - **That fix moved the FLOOR of the ramp and left the CEILING exactly where it was, which is worth recording because it looked done and wasn't (code review, 2026-09-09).** Both bands top out at `STATURE_STRENGTH_HI` (ovr 89), so an 89+ free agent still scored a flat 1.0 while the strongest club in a fresh 626-club world measures **0.763** — at full care he needed 0.82 and there was none, on a world whose best players are **90-91**. Every club including his own former one refused him, and the Manual's new "build the club up and he'll take your call" line was unreachable advice for that band. `freeAgentFromStature` now caps ability at **the world's own maximum stature**, so the best player alive always has somewhere by construction and it self-adjusts as clubs grow, with no tuned ceiling. It binds only above 89, so every rating below behaves exactly as measured. **The test that should have caught it did not, because its fixture was unrealistic**: a squad of 88s on hype 90 measures ~0.94, above the uncapped 1.0−0.18 bar, so the bug could not reproduce. Lowering the fixture club to a realistic 0.767 makes **four** tests fail without the fix instead of one.
  - **A `max(lastClub, ability)` makes re-signing your own player impossible by construction, and that is ordinary play rather than an edge case.** Ability outranks the club whenever the player outgrew it, so a median-stature club that develops an ovr-85 academy graduate and lets his contract lapse at offseason step 1 could **never take him back** — while an AI club, which is not gated at all, would. Hence the explicit "nobody refuses to stay where he already was" exemption on `buyerTid === lastClubTid`. Forgetting to extend is why the "Extend all" button exists.
  - **The refusal must be applied BEFORE the render cap, not per row — this repeated a lesson already in this file.** `FreeAgents.tsx` ranks by `ovr + ceiling` and slices to `MAX_LISTED`, and refusals land exactly on the highest-rated players, so computing `refuses` at render time let them eat the whole 25-row list from the top and hide every signable player below the cut, on a page with no pagination. Verbatim the loan-in panel's documented failure ("the top 40 borrowable players are refused every time"). Refused rows are now **sunk in the sort** rather than dropped, so the reason stays visible when there is room for it.
  - **Measured against the reported save's real signings**: blocked at the level they were made are the 85, 83, 81 and 79; the 76-78 squad filler goes through; and every one of them is signable now the club is genuinely 2nd-biggest in France (stature 0.617). That gradient is the design — it stops ladder-skipping without punishing a club that has climbed.
  - **`runAIFreeAgency` takes the same gate, and leaving it out was the sharpest thing the review found (2026-09-09).** All three passes now filter candidates through `refusesFreeAgentSigningWith`. Without it the AI was the one actor in the world exempt from the module that exists to stop stars moving downhill — and worse than exempt, because `freeAgencySigningOrder` is **worst-first**: the weakest club in the world got first pick of every elite free agent while the user was refused the identical signing. The pass most likely to move a star down the pyramid was the one pass that never asked him. It also made `refusesFreeAgentSigning`'s own docstring false where it says "this binds the user exactly as it binds an AI club"; now it is true. Statures are computed **once** per call rather than after each signing — recomputing would be quadratic over 626 clubs and buys nothing, since a club's stature is a top-16 mean blended with hype and one arrival barely moves it. Nearly free for a real pool, because `statureSensitivity` returns 0 below the care floor and that is the bulk of what is on offer (prospects included, so the prospect pass is unaffected in practice).
    - **It also made the feature better rather than merely more consistent, and the tell is a column that used to read the same three times.** With the AI ungated the mop-up removed everything the gate would have blocked, so "best signable" came out **79/79/79** for a weak, mid and big club alike — the user-side gate was inert in a fresh dynasty. Gated, an occasional elite free agent now survives the mop-up (only a big club will take him, and it may not need him) and the column reads **79/79/85**: the stature gradient is visible for the first time, which is the whole point of the gate. Pooled 4-seed block spreads also *widen* — BIG4→Serbia **+9.35 → +10.00**, France→Serbia **+4.92 → +5.80** — which is stars no longer draining to the bottom of the pyramid.
    - **Writing the test for this is where the traps are, and two fixtures were vacuous before one worked.** Removing a player from a roster to create the free agent leaves his club a man **short at that position**, and the shortfall pass runs for every club *before* the poach pass runs for any — so his old club signs him straight back and the poach pass, the thing under test, never sees him. Measured: the released player landed at the strongest club via pass 1 regardless of the gate. The fixture must **add** a free agent rather than release one. (The first attempt also happened to release a GK, which made the shortfall certain.)

Pure, zero rng draws. `refusesFreeAgentSigningWith` is the precomputed-stature form for listing pages, because the object form rebuilds a league-wide player index per call — the quadratic trap `clubStatures` exists to avoid.

  - **Retention is ADDITIVE to the ovr depth chart, never folded into its ranking, and that is the whole safety argument.** Blending potential into the trim is the obvious implementation and it trades starters for prospects — a club keeping a 30-ovr 16-year-old *instead of* a 55-ovr squad player fields a worse XI, moving match quality, the M1 benchmark gates and the country ladder together. Anyone reasoning about intake throughput from the sign limit will be out by half.

    - **POT predicts peak WELL at a strong academy and barely at all at a weak one — `r = +0.48` (England) against `+0.14` (Serbia), pooled over every graduate.** The mechanism is `YOUTH_BASE_FLOOR`: a weak academy's `youthGenerationBase` is saturated on the softplus floor (measured: **63% of second-division clubs and 100% of third-division ones**, and the bottom countries' top flights partly too), so its prospects are all generated off near-identical inputs and the POT *differences* between them are mostly estimate noise rather than real differences. The scouting fog is therefore worth far more to a big-four academy than to a weak one, which is the opposite of the intuition that a poor club needs its scouts more.
      - **Do NOT compute this correlation on a top-N slice.** A first pass took the top 15 graduates of 21, already sorted by peak, and read `+0.10` — truncating a sample on the dependent variable attenuates the correlation toward zero, and here it did so by a factor of nearly five. The probe pools every graduate for exactly this reason, and the same trap applies to any "best players" list in this repo.
    - **Two harness bugs this cost, both worth recognising on sight.** (a) **The user's club is unmanaged headlessly, so nothing renews ITS contracts** — `releaseExpiredContracts` drops each graduate the moment his deal lapses, he lands in free agency, stops playing, and progression's `minutesFactor` stalls him. The first run reported peaks depressed by the harness, with a graduate reading peak 76 / now 63 at age 25. Any headless probe following a user squad over seasons must call `extendContracts` every summer, which is what the "Extend all" button does. (b) **Attributing intake to the club that KEPT a player measures survivors, not production**: `trimRosterSurplus` releases most of an intake the same summer and selects on ovr *and* now on potential, so a first attempt at "mean POT by division" read a nearly flat 63.0/60.7/61.6 across the three tiers when generation-time truth is **54.9/47.2/45.3**. Measure at generation.
  - **Ordinary filler is still released** (intake released 84-86% → 71-73%): the potential bar sits well above the ~62 median intake potential, so a club spends its slots on genuine wonderkids rather than every teenager. `AI_PROSPECT_MAX_AGE` is `PROSPECT_AGE_MAX` **by reference** — the same "still a prospect" line Incoming Talent draws, so the AI cannot stop protecting players the user is still shown as prospects.
- **Players choose where they sign, and leagues keep their nationality mix (PR #401, 2026-09-24, Stage 1 of `docs/club-reputation.md`, which is the full record).** On `main` every league drifted to 3-20% domestic within 20 seasons (Argentina 84% → 19%) because nothing ever read where a player came from and free agency let the worst club pick first. Three parts:
  - **`clubAppealFor` (`transfers/clubAppeal.ts`)**: a player's view of a club as additive lines (level of club, the only one that can refuse; playing time; home country; former club), one computation read by the AI markets (`appealScore`) and the screens. **The home line (`homePull.ts`, `APPEAL_HOME` 0.25) is what keeps leagues domestic**, scaled by the league's real domestic share and by `homeAttachment`, which fades for stars and for a player above his country's stage (pinned to the ladder via `APPEAL_HOME_LEVEL_PER_OFFSET`, NOT to the league's own squads: that fed itself). **`APPEAL_CONFEDERATION` is 0 on purpose** (the line is kept for a language-corridor version): at 0.15 it kept France/Portugal/Belgium too domestic and taxed every South American move to Europe twice.
  - **Free agency is club-proposing deferred acceptance** (`freeAgencyMatch.ts`): clubs offer, each free agent keeps the offer he likes best. Zero shared-rng draws (per-signing seeded streams, tags 7/5/6; `freeAgencyRng.test.ts`). Deterministic; stable and order-independent except at a registration cap.
  - **Real registration rules (`foreignRules.ts`)** in each league's real shape, only where the real league has one, binding the user too. **Caps are hard** on every acquisition path including the academy (a club over one is frozen, never culled); **minimums are soft** (preferred in signings, never broken by the trim). Youth intake and `ensureUserRosterSafety` are exempt. A league the player ADDED (it carries its own `nationalities`) takes no shipped rules even under a real country's name. MLS's real slots count green-card holders as domestic, so it has none here (by nationality the cap froze all 30 clubs).
  - **Tools**: `scripts/nationalityDriftProbe.ts` (domestic share per league over N seasons, flows by route, supply of nationals, clubs at a cap) and `scripts/statureGapProbe.ts`.
  - **Measured, 20 seasons, 4 seeds pooled by hand against `origin/main`**: every league within ~8.5 points of its real domestic share (mean absolute gap 3.5-4.4, leaning ~2 points foreign); **solvent on 4/4, zero clubs in deficit** (main dips on 2/4). **Every ladder rung passes the mean gate except France→Brazil (−1.27)**: Brazil keeps its good players and ends level with or above France. - **Evaluation core** — `core/ai/clubContext.ts` (`deriveLeagueContexts`: squad strength/age/depth + league-normalized **ambition**/**frugality** scalars, *derived from state, not stored*) and `evaluate.ts` (`valueToClub` layers positional-need/timeline/affordability on `trueTransferValue`; `perceivedValueToClub` adds scouting-noise scaled by frugality). Normalization is scoped **within each competition** — *except* `stature` (see below), which is deliberately world-wide.
- **Buy-side vs keep-side valuation (`ValuationSide`, 2026-08-08) — load-bearing, was the single worst realism bug.** A club must not price its own players with the buy-side formula: that formula asks *how much would he upgrade us*, so a club's own best player at a position scores **zero** (he *is* `posBestOvr` — he's measured against himself), the depth term reads *surplus* because he's one of the bodies, and affordability marks him down again for a purchase that isn't happening. Net effect: **the worse a club was, the more it valued a star**, so a superstar's own club was always his *lowest* valuer and he was permanently cheap + available. Measured on a real world: an 85-ovr player's own club (squad 75.0) valued him at **$132M** against a $350M market, while the five highest bidders were the *weakest* clubs alive (a squad-51.9 side valued him at **$538M**). `keepValueToClub` fixes it counterfactually — "how far would we fall back without him": incumbent = `posSecondBestOvr` (new `ClubContext` field) when he's the club's best, depth counted as `depth-1`, **no affordability**, and timeline takes `max(prime, youth)` so a win-now club can't write off its own 21-year-old. Used for every *reservation* price (`runAITransferMarket`, `inboundOffers`, both `loans.ts` sites). **Renewals deliberately stay buy-side** — `AI_RENEWAL_MARGIN` is calibrated against those numbers, and keep-side values re-sign essentially everyone and *starve free agency* (measured: `runAIFreeAgency` signed nobody at all). Revisit only with `AI_RENEWAL_MARGIN` retuned in the same change.
- **Player will (`core/transfers/playerWill.ts`, 2026-08-08)** — the missing *other half* of a transfer. Every valuation is a club's view; nothing represented whether the **player** wants the move, which is why stars poured downhill. Football has no salary cap to justify a star at a small club (basketball does — cap space genuinely sends stars to small markets), so this supplies the constraint. `ClubContext.stature` ([0,1], squad strength vs an **absolute** band blended with hype — the one normalization that is *not* per-competition, because "is this a step up" is a global question and the biggest club in a weak league is still small). `moveAppeal` scales a **buyer's** valuation by how much the player likes the destination, weighted by `statureSensitivity` (a ≤62-ovr squad player is indifferent; a ≥78 star weighs it fully); a big enough step down is a flat `refusesMove`. `settledMultiplier` scales a **reservation** up for a player who just arrived, decaying over `PLAYER_SETTLED_SEASONS` — `joinedSeasons` derives arrival from `league.transfers` (loans/loan-returns excluded), so **no persisted field and no migration**. **Pure functions, zero rng draws**, and both market gates sit **after** the jitter draw (same rule as the Division-2 guard) so RNG stream order is untouched. Applies to the **user identically** (`refusesMoveToClub` in `makeTransferOffer`/`acceptCounterOffer`/`recommendedTransfers`/world search's `notForSaleReason`) — exempting the user would just hand them the exploit.
- **AI↔AI market** (`core/ai/transferMarket.ts`) — a player's reservation is his value to his current club; he moves to whoever values him enough more to clear it and afford the fee. Guardrails: min-surplus, per-window buy/sell caps, depth floor, cash reserve. Runs once per window. **There is deliberately no availability screen — protection is by price, not veto (2026-08-11).** `AI_MARKET_AVAILABILITY` used to skip any player whose keep-side reservation exceeded his club-blind market value; those quantities aren't commensurable, and keep-side is *built* to exceed market for a club's own best player, so of 239 clubs the number whose best player could ever be offered was **zero**. That shut off the upward drain of talent that counteracts `growthDamping`, and the strength ladder inverted outright — England finished *below* Portugal over 20 seasons on shipped main. An irreplaceable player is now protected by a high reservation the buyer must clear, not by exclusion. **Any replacement prune must bound the best possible buyer valuation against the reservation; comparing to market value reintroduces the bug.** Cost of having no prune: one market run is ~52ms → ~357ms on the **240-club** world it was measured on (`scripts/marketTiming.ts`), in the worker. **It scales worse than linearly with world size, so re-measure after adding a country rather than reusing the number** (it was also originally mislabelled "320-club" here): the pre-filter was the only early-out ahead of the O(teams) buyer loop, so cost grows with candidates × clubs. **Measured on the 16-competition/320-club world: ~1611ms per run, 560 deals** — 4.5× the 240-club figure for a 1.33× club count. At two windows a season that is ~3.2s per season against ~0.1s when the filter existed, roughly **9% of a season-cycle's sim time** (~34s) versus well under 1% before. Still in the worker, so it does not block the main thread, but this is now the strongest argument for a *sound* replacement prune — which must bound the best possible buyer valuation against the reservation, never compare to market value. Full diagnosis: `docs/transfer-mobility.md`. **Need-buy path** (`AI_NEED_BUY_*`, `hasPositionalGap` in `evaluate.ts`): the normal `AI_MARKET_MIN_SURPLUS` gate only fires on *bargains*, which left cash-rich clubs banking money while a position went unfilled. When a buyer has a genuine positional gap there (understaffed vs `ROSTER_COMPOSITION`, or a weak startable hole this player upgrades), its required surplus margin drops to `AI_NEED_BUY_MIN_SURPLUS` (0 = pay a fair price, no discount) and it frees up part of its frugality-driven reserve (`AI_NEED_BUY_RESERVE_RELIEF`, never below `AI_MARKET_RESERVE_FRACTION_MIN` → no deficit). Elites stay unbuyable (priceless-star premium is in valuation, untouched); it only routes affordable, already-available squad players to clubs that need them (a move, not a creation → no inflation risk). **The need-buy path is not an exception — it is 92-93% of all executed deals, and that is load-bearing (measured 2026-08-12, `scripts/needBuyMarginProbe.ts`).** `hasPositionalGap`'s understaffed branch tests `posDepth < ROSTER_COMPOSITION`, a *stocking* target that sums to 25 against squads trimmed to 25, so a squad not distributed exactly to target is short somewhere and essentially every club always is. That reads as a loose predicate; it was tightened two ways and **both were rejected**. Depth (a hole = "cannot field the position", half the target — which is also `keepsDepthFloor`'s threshold) hit its target cleanly, 92%→34% with the ladder green on all four seeds, and put **2 of 4 seeds into deficit** (Belgium −£2.7M s16, Turkey −£1.9M s17) where main has none: Belgium lost a third of its *sales* but two thirds of its *income*, because a strong club buying a weak league's star qualifies via *understaffed* (it is strong everywhere, so the weak-starter branch never fires for it) and depth is quality-blind, so it cut the valuable upward sales along with the filler. A quality gate instead (a shortage counts only if the player addresses it) was principled and **inert**, 92.3%→91.5%, since a buyer only bids on players it already rates. **The generalisation, which outlives this predicate: the weak leagues run on transfer receipts** — Belgium's 20 clubs take ~£1.1bn in a *single window* against ~£800M of base income for the whole league across a season (`BASE_SEASON_BUDGET` × `COUNTRY_BUDGET_SCALE`) — so **anything that reduces market volume lands on the same tripwire**, including the sound buyer-loop prune wanted for performance, a need-to-sell term, and a floor on `AI_NEED_BUY_MIN_SURPLUS`. Two process notes: **deal count is the wrong yardstick, receipts into the poor leagues is the right one** (dropping a hundred £1M filler deals is harmless, dropping a few big upward sales is not, and counts can't tell them apart — the probe prints receipts by league for exactly this); and **the finance column fails first, not the ladder** — the change that bankrupted two seeds passed every ladder check, so watching only strength would have shipped it. Full diagnosis: `docs/transfer-mobility.md`.
- **A player changes clubs at most ONCE per transfer window (`movedThisWindow`, `core/transfers/negotiation.ts`, 2026-09-09, user report).** Football has no mid-window resale market the way the North American leagues trade, and without the rule a club buys cheap and sells on at many times the fee inside one window, banking the difference for holding a player zero matchdays. The predicate reads the transfer log for the open `(season, window)` and gates the AI market (by **seeding** its `moved` set, never by filtering candidates — every jitter draw happens during candidate assembly, so a skip up there shifts the stream), `makeTransferOffer`, `acceptCounterOffer`, `inboundOfferCandidates`, `recommendedTransfers` and `saleGateFor`. Derived, so **no persisted field and no migration**. Free-agent legs are excluded (they carry `faTransferLocked`'s longer hold, and FA *departures* are never logged, so counting the sentinel would lock half a story); loans are excluded in both directions, since a loan moves who a player turns out for rather than who owns him.
  - **The bug was NOT in the market, and that is the whole lesson: the AI market's own `moved` set only spans ONE RUN of ONE FUNCTION, while a window spans several mechanisms.** `enforceDivisionCeilings` logs summer-window moves immediately before *and* after the market inside `simOffseason`, and the user then trades through matchday 4 of the same window. `AI_MARKET_FEE_FLOOR_FRACTION`'s comment says it exists "so a lowballed seller can't be flipped for many times the fee he was just sold for" — it could not hold, because it bounds one deal against the player's market value while the two legs of a chain came from *different mechanisms* and neither ever saw the other's fee.
  - **Measured before the fix (`scripts/windowDoubleMoveProbe.ts`, seed 1, 5 seasons): 6 → 34 → 27 → 46 → 58 extra in-window moves per summer, ~3-4% of a window's ~1,500 deals and GROWING; winter clean; zero A→B→A flip-backs.** Chains ran **$7.1M → $113.3M**, **$10.0M → $70.4M**, **$13.9M → $61.3M**. After: **0 in every window of every season**, deal volume unchanged.
  - **Two causes, and neither was the one the shape tally suggested — read the ATTRIBUTION note before trusting a heuristic here.** Labelling a leg "sweep" vs "market" from end-of-run tiers is worthless (promotion moves clubs between the event and the reading) and pointed at the wrong mechanism twice. What settled it was making the sweep stamp its transfers `window: "winter"` for one throwaway run: every *summer* bucket then read 0, i.e. the market and user paths were already clean and every remaining chain was two sweep legs. **Tag the producer, don't infer it from the product.**
  - **Cause 1: `runAITransferMarket`'s tier guard was still `tier === 2` against `DIVISION_2_REFUSAL_OVR_THRESHOLD`, and was the one buy path left behind by third divisions (#308).** `inboundOffers.ts` and `runAILoanMarket` were both generalized to `divisionRefusalOvr(tier)` then; the market was not, so **tier-3 buyers were entirely unguarded** and the market sold over-ceiling players into third divisions all summer for the post-market sweep to haul straight back. Fixing it alone took the count 171-202 → **85** and stopped it growing. **The existing test could not catch it**: it asserted "no ≥70 player joins a *tier-2* club", which is vacuous for exactly the tier nobody remembered to add — it now asserts against `divisionRefusalOvr(t.toTid's tier)` for **every** tier, plus a non-vacuity check that tier-3 clubs buy at all.
  - **Cause 2: `enforceDivisionCeilings` hoisted a multi-ceiling player a rung at a time and logged a transfer PER RUNG.** A third-division player good enough for the top flight read as two transfers in one window, an intermediate club banked the difference between a second-tier fee and a top-flight one, and if that club sat at `ROSTER_CAP` it released one of its own players to make room for someone it lost again in the same breath. `landingTierFor(ovr, fromTier)` now walks to the deepest division whose ceiling he does *not* clear and he moves there in **one** move, at one fee, from his real club to his real destination. **Skipping the intermediate provably cannot change where he lands** — the receiving pool is drawn from the SELLER'S COUNTRY, which never changes on the way up, and the neediest-club search reads only the destination tier's rosters, so the intermediate's identity never entered the next rung's decision. What does change is *ordering* (a double-clearer now reaches tier 1 during the tier-3 pass rather than after every tier-3 hoist has landed), so which tier-1 club receives whom moves.
  - **`enforceDivisionCeilings` remains exempt as a MOVER while its moves still LOCK**, and the file says so at length so nobody "fixes" the asymmetry: it must be able to confiscate a player the market has just sold below the ceiling *in the same offseason*, which is the entire reason the second call sits after the market.
  - **A persisted inbound offer skipped every gate, which is pre-existing and had teeth.** `resolveOpenOffer` returns the stored row before consulting `inboundOfferCandidates`, so every sale condition — borrowed, out on loan, moved this window — was checked only when a FRESH offer was built. `acceptCounterOffer` on the buy side already re-checks its own conditions ("the roster may have changed since the counter was made"); `canStillSell` is the missing mirror. The loan case is the one that bites: nothing in `loans.ts` clears `inboundOffers`, so a player can go out on loan with an open offer standing, and `executeTransfer` removes a pid only from the *seller's* roster — the user's, where he no longer is — while pushing him onto the buyer's, leaving **one pid on two rosters with a live loan pointing at a third club** for `processLoanReturns` to duplicate again.
  - **Audited `weakLeaguesAudit.ts`, 20 seasons × SIX seeds per side, against `origin/main`. Ladder clean, solvency 6/6 → 5/6.** Every rung mean inside ±1 on both sides; resolvable rungs BIG4→France **+3.91 → +3.97**, France→Netherlands +2.67 → +2.30, Netherlands→Portugal +1.68 → +1.09; block spreads BIG4→Serbia **10.06 → 9.74** (gate ≥4) and France→Serbia **6.16 → 5.77** (gate ≥1). - **Transfer clauses: sell-on shares and bonuses** (`core/transfers/clauses.ts`, `LeagueStore.transferClauses`, 2026-09-03) — contingent money on a deal the **user** negotiates, either direction. A sell-on pays a share of the buyer's **profit** over what he paid (so a resale at a loss pays nothing); a bonus pays once on one of four triggers (25 league appearances or 10 league goals in a season, the buyer qualifying for a continental competition, the buyer being promoted).
  - **Add-ons sit ON TOP of the cash, and the cash you name is what changes hands (2026-09-03, user call).** `clauseExpectedValue` is added to your side of the scales before the other club decides: buying, offer + value is judged against the seller's reservation; selling, ask + value against the buyer's ceiling. **One function prices both directions** — if buy and sell ever disagreed, one of them is free money, which is this feature's entire exploit surface (same argument that put `saleGateFor` in one place). The risk direction is **under**-pricing, since that clears a reservation too cheaply; `scripts/clausePricingProbe.ts` measures priced expected value against what a clause would actually have paid out over a simmed dynasty.
    - **It shipped the other way round first, and the reversal is worth understanding.** The original model treated the other club's willingness as fixed and took the add-on value *out* of the cash, so attaching a sell-on lowered the fee. That is defensible economics — the same expected total, split differently — and it is **nobody's mental model of a transfer**, which is how the user put it: real deals are reported as "£20m rising to £25m", add-ons on top. The tell was the panel having to say "$3.0M of the $43.0M moved into add-ons", a sentence a reader has to reverse-engineer. Under the current model add-ons do the thing real add-ons do: **close a deal the cash alone could not reach.**
    - **The exploit the old model closed by construction now needs a bound, and that bound is the ceiling.** If cash never moves, then selling with a sell-on is the full fee *plus* the upside, i.e. free money. `buyerAcceptsClauses` is what stops it: a buyer wears add-ons on top of his own standing offer only while offer + value stays under `inboundCeiling`, otherwise accepting is a no-op. The Incoming Offers page calls the same predicate to grey the button out rather than leaving it inert — and `inboundCeiling` is shared with `counterInboundOffer` so accepting and countering cannot disagree about what he can afford.
  - **It must NOT live on `CompletedTransfer`, and that is a correctness requirement that fails SILENTLY.** `detachTransfers` windows the transfer log to the last `PLAYER_SETTLED_SEASONS` (3) seasons before the league crosses to the worker — and a bonus settles, and a resale fires a sell-on, *inside* that worker. A clause agreed in season 3 and triggered in season 9 would read as absent: no crash, no type error, the beneficiary just never gets paid. Its own never-detached field instead, and `clauses.test.ts` pins the round trip through all four detaches so a later "tidy-up" onto the log fails a test rather than shipping a save that quietly stops paying. It is also the wrong *shape* for the log, which is append-only history where an obligation is live state that gets deleted.
  - **Bounded without a cap.** A clause dies on payout, on expiry, when the player leaves the club that owes it, and when he leaves the world. The last two matter most: departures other than a sale (release, contract expiry, the division-ceiling sweep) are caught by **one ownership prune at offseason step 6.7** rather than by clause code at every departure site — and that prune must treat a **loaned-out player as still owned**, since he sits on the loanee's roster while belonging to his parent. Retirement and the free-agent cull scrub it alongside `watchlist`, at the same two sites.
  - **Two settlement points, both pure.** Sales go through `settleClausesOnSale`, called from the **single shared fee path** — `executeTransfer` *and* `runAITransferMarket`, which credits budgets inline rather than going through it. That second call site is the load-bearing one: the club that bought the player is usually an AI club, so his resale is an AI↔AI deal and is where the user's sell-on actually fires. Bonuses go through `settleBonuses` at **offseason step 6.7**, which sits *after* `cupRoutes` because two triggers aren't answerable earlier (promotion is decided at 3.6, continental qualification needs those routes) — so bonus money lands after the new season's wages are charged, which is the right way round for an add-on earned last season.
  - **`executeTransfer` moves money through a delta map, not a chain of tid tests**, because one club can legitimately be two parties at once (sell a player on with a sell-on, buy him back later, and the buyer is also the beneficiary). It preserves the old behaviour exactly where it matters: the **seller is clamped unconditionally** rather than only on a positive delta, which is what that function did before clauses existed, so a zero-fee move can't quietly change.
  - **User-only, so no dynasty audit is involved** — AI↔AI deals attach no clauses, world money flow is byte-identical to a save without the feature, and the shipped transfer tests pass unchanged. Same property every difficulty lever has and worth preserving: **giving AI clubs clauses of their own changes receipts into the weak leagues**, which is this file's most-repeated tripwire, and would need `weakLeaguesAudit.ts` on both sides.
  - **Every constant here is measured, and the first guesses were badly wrong in the direction that matters.** `scripts/clausePricingProbe.ts` replays a simmed dynasty's real transfers and asks, per deal, what `clauseValue` would have quoted against what the clause would actually have paid. First run, 24,394 club-to-club sales over 10 seasons: **`realized / priced` = 0.556**, i.e. the user was handing over roughly twice the cash a sell-on was worth. The two obvious constants were already right — **46.5%** of players really are resold inside the 5-season window (`SELL_ON_RESALE_PROBABILITY` 0.45) at a mean **1.88** seasons (`SELL_ON_PRICING_HORIZON` 2) — and the gap was in **what for**: only **40.1%** of resales are at a profit at all, while `projectedValue` marches straight toward potential and ignores `growthDamping` throttling exactly the players a clause gets attached to. Hence `SELL_ON_PROFIT_REALIZATION` (0.55), which scales the profit term rather than bending the projection (that projection is an honest read of what the valuation curve says he'd be worth, and it is the same curve the resale will be priced on). The bonus rates were worse and took **two** passes, the second of which is the interesting one. `BONUS_BASE_PROBABILITY` shipped at 0.35/0.2 per season against measured **39.5% / 8.4% over three seasons** — appearances priced at better than even money, goals at ~7x their true rate. Setting them to the measured *population* rate (15.4% / 2.9% per season) still read **0.754 / 0.695**, because `triggerProbability` multiplies by a logistic centred on an even match with the incumbent starter, and **clubs buy players who are better than the man they displace**: the population mean of that quality term is **1.42**, not 1.0, so quality was being counted twice. The constants are therefore the even-match rate (0.109 / 0.020), not the population rate. The two team triggers needed a different fix — the slot-count model is the right *shape* (it varies correctly by country and division) and assumes he stays for the whole window, measuring **60.7% predicted vs 23.8% realized** for continental and **36.2% vs 14.7%** for promotion, i.e. **0.392 and 0.406**. Landing on the same number is what identifies the cause as the shared one, tenure, hence one `BONUS_TEAM_TRIGGER_REALIZATION` (0.4) rather than two fudges — and it is deliberately NOT applied to the performance triggers, whose bases already have attrition inside them. **An over-priced bonus is not a mild bug — it is one the user should rationally never take**, because the cash it costs exceeds what it is worth. Re-run the probe after touching any of them; every row reads 1.0 when the model is honest. **Caveat worth stating: this is 24,394 deals but ONE seed**, so the sampling error inside the world is negligible while the world itself is a single draw — a second seed is the cheap check before treating 0.55 as exact, and this file has burned itself twice before generalising from one seed (see the `staysSolvent` gate and the position-change audit).
    - **Two traps inside the probe itself, both worth knowing.** `CupState.teams` is the **knockout bracket** (8 clubs, `-1` in unfilled slots), not the qualified field — reading it reported *0.0%* of buyers ever reaching Europe; the field is `leaguePhase.teams`. And a team trigger's rate has to be measured against the clubs it is even **possible** for: the model scores promotion at exactly 0 for a top-flight buyer and continental qualification at 0 below the top flight, so pooling all buyers into one denominator reports a rate the model was never trying to predict.
  - **The bonus menu and its thresholds are PER PLAYER (`suggestedBonuses`, 2026-09-03), and that forced the probability model to become threshold-aware.** A menu identical for everyone is not a suggestion, and the shipped one was worse than that: it offered a goals bonus for a keeper and a promotion bonus to a top-flight club, both unable to fire. Two things now vary. **(a) Which bonuses appear falls out of the pricing rather than a hardcoded list of who may score** — a keeper's chance of reaching any sensible tally is nil so no goal bonus is suggested, and the two team triggers are dropped in the half of the pyramid where `triggerProbability` already scores them zero. **(b) The threshold is set where the payout is about as likely as any other suggestion** (`BONUS_SUGGESTION_TARGET_P` 0.35), so a fringe signing is offered a modest games target and a first-choice one a demanding target: equal ambition, different numbers. `BonusClause.threshold` is stored per clause, which was free to add because no save has ever held a clause.
    - **The probability HAS to respond to the threshold — that is correctness, not polish.** A 5-goal bonus priced like a 15-goal one is free money at the low end, the same exploit the sell-on pricing exists to close arriving through the bonus door. Appearances are a normal on a saturating share curve, goals a Poisson on a per-position rate, both fit to **25,087 measured player-seasons at the club that bought them**. `clauses.test.ts` pins monotonicity directly (probability never rises as the target does) rather than trusting the formula.
    - **Measuring corrected both halves of the guess substantially.** Appearance share **saturates**: by ovr edge over the incumbent starter it runs 0.336 / 0.398 / 0.497 / 0.766 / 0.680 / 0.725 across the buckets below −10 through 10+, so a plain logistic over-predicted the top end badly (0.855 against a real 0.725) — and that is where most signings sit, 64% of observations being in the 10+ bucket. Being *much* better than the man you displace buys very little over being somewhat better, because there are only so many games. Goals per appearance came out **GK 0 / CB 0.023 / FB 0.037 / DM 0.075 / CM 0.156 / AM 0.279 / W 0.274 / ST 0.550** — every placeholder too low, and FB and DM inverted. The 24x spread from centre-back to striker dwarfs anything ovr does within a position, which is why `BONUS_GOAL_OVR_SLOPE` is gentle.
    - **The tenure bug, worth reading because I caused it and the reasoning is the trap.** After the model was rebuilt, the counting triggers measured **0.512 and 0.426** — priced at roughly double. The per-season model was fine; the *compounding* was not. `triggerProbability` raised it over the full 3-season window, and **a bought player is still at that club for only 1.03 of those seasons on average** (25,087 player-seasons over 24,394 transfers), with a bonus dying when he leaves. `BONUS_EXPECTED_SEASONS_AT_CLUB` is that exponent. What made it easy to miss: the performance triggers had been *deliberately exempted* from the tenure correction, on the correct grounds that their old `BONUS_BASE_PROBABILITY` entries were fit against realized window rates and so already contained attrition — and that premise vanished when those constants were replaced by a per-season distribution model. **A justification can outlive the thing that justified it**, and only re-measuring catches it. The two team triggers keep their own factor: `slots / size` is crude on its own, so `BONUS_TEAM_TRIGGER_REALIZATION` is absorbing tenure *and* that crudeness at once, and re-splitting them would mean re-fitting it for nothing.
    - **Final measured state, all five in band** (24,394 deals, seed 1): sell-on **1.010**, appearances **1.035**, goals **0.861**, continental **0.919**, promotion **0.980**. Goals is the weakest and is mildly over-priced, which is the safe direction. The suggestions really do differ: appearance targets span **9-35** (mean 27.5) and goal targets **3-19** (mean 6.9), and a goal bonus is offered on **11,322 of 24,394** deals — keepers, centre-backs and full-backs drop off the menu on their own arithmetic.
    - **The suggested threshold targets a probability, so it is unaffected by the on-top reversal** — the threshold search and the pricing both read the same per-season model, and only what the number is *compared against* changed.
    - **Known limit, and it is what confines the design to suggestions.** Both real distributions are over-dispersed against the model (a lump at zero: a player either holds a place or does not), so the tail is over-predicted at low thresholds and under-predicted at high ones. That is tolerable **only** because a threshold is suggested rather than typed: every one the game offers sits near the target probability, in the middle of the distribution where the fit is good, and the probe verifies **at the thresholds actually suggested** rather than on a fixed grid. Letting a user name an arbitrary threshold — the option deliberately not taken — needs a negative binomial first.
  - **`switchClub` deliberately does NOT clear it**, for the reason `activeLoans` survives there: a clause is a contract between two *clubs*, not an instruction from the manager, so the club he left keeps what it is owed and keeps owing what it owes. Same for `beginAutopilot`. Both files say so, so neither gets "tidied" into the clear list.
  - **Zero rng draws anywhere**, so no scoreline moves and the `touchStats` hash is untouched. New `LeagueStore` field migrated to `[]` and never backfilled: a save written before this agreed no clauses, so empty is exact.
  - **Known gap, deliberately left: a payout arrives silently.** When a sell-on fires or a bonus triggers, the money lands in the budget and the row disappears from the Finance ledger, and nothing announces it. It genuinely cannot be derived after the fact — the clause is deleted the moment it settles, which is what keeps the list bounded — so this is the one case in this feature that would need a real persisted `NewsEvent`, exactly on the test `newsEvents.ts` states (persist only what can't be derived). The payouts already flow out of `settleClausesOnSale`/`settleBonuses` as `ClausePayout[]`, so the work is plumbing them back through the market and the offseason the way `culledPids` is reported, plus one event type. It was left out because a new event type is a one-way door (removing one later means migrating it out of every save's append-only feed), and this PR was already large.
  - UI: a shared `ClauseEditor` on both negotiation surfaces (Transfers and Incoming Offers) which quotes what changes hands now and what it rises to if the add-ons land, in the terms a transfer is really reported in; a `ClauseLedger` card on Finance listing both directions; a per-player note on the profile. Caps are `SELL_ON_MAX_SHARE` (0.4) and `BONUS_MAX_TOTAL_FRACTION` (0.5 of the fee), which also bound how far a deliberately coarse pricing model can be leaned on.
- **Contract renewals** (`core/ai/renewals.ts`) — final-year AI players renewed if value ≥ salary × margin (offseason step 0). Sell-at-peak/replace-aging are emergent (no separate mechanic).
- **News Feed** (`/news`) — transfer activity + player accomplishments. Accomplishments (`core/newsEvents.ts`, hat-trick/standout/goal-milestones/position-change) detected at sim time into append-only `LeagueStore.newsEvents` (can't be derived retroactively). Timeline merge in `ui/newsFeedTimeline.ts`. **Removing an event type means migrating it out of existing saves** (the feed is append-only, so old rows survive and would render a blank label) — see the generational-talent removal under Extremism.
  - **Relevance is tiered, because a world feed is ~90% news about countries the user doesn't play in (2026-08-28).** Measured on 16 competitions / 320 clubs, seed 7 season 5: **4,752 rows a season, 11.4% of them touching the user's own league**, median subject OVR **61** (below an average starter). `buildSeasonTimeline` now takes a `NewsAudience` and sorts by reach: the user's club shows everything (loans, loan returns, free signings included); their competition shows every detected accomplishment plus every paid deal and loan; elsewhere only `newsEventScope(e) === "world"` and paid deals ≥ `NEWS_WORLD_TRANSFER_FEE`. Result **897 rows, 46.0% own-league**, same seed and world. `scripts/newsRelevanceProbe.ts` prints both columns and has a `BASELINE=1` mode; **read volume and relevance together** — cutting rows by dropping the user's own news would look like a win on volume alone.
  - **It scales with the world, which is the property that makes adding countries cheap here (measured 2026-08-29, 24 competitions / 420 clubs, same seed 7 season 5).** Growing the world makes the underlying problem *worse* — untiered the feed goes **4,752 rows / 11.4% own-league → 5,838 / 8.9%** — and the tiered feed barely moves: **897 / 46.0% → 965 / 41.6%**, i.e. **+7.6% rows for a 31% bigger world**. The foreign tier is fee-gated rather than count-gated, so it absorbs new countries almost entirely, and what a reader sees is set by their own league's size rather than the world's. Worth re-running the probe after any country addition, but the expected result is that volume holds and own-league share drifts down a few points — a *large* move in either column would mean a new event type is reaching the world tier.
  - **The tier is DERIVED from the event's type and detail, never stored on it.** No new persisted field, no migration, and the tiers apply retroactively to events older builds already wrote. Same reason `isNewsworthy` re-applies the detection floor at render: a save from before the ladder existed is full of every-10 milestones that would otherwise crowd its feed forever.
  - **Goal milestones climb a ladder (career 50 then every 50, season 25 then every 5), and the flat every-10 step was the single biggest source of both feed rows and save growth** — 1,661 career milestones a season by season 4, mostly journeymen passing 60. `newsEvents` is append-only and persisted forever, so **detection volume is a permanent save cost, distinct from what the feed draws**; raising the bar at detection is what shrinks the save, tiering only shrinks the page. Detection is pure and takes **no shared-`rng` draw**, so scorelines are bit-identical either way.
  - **Career and season milestones are deduped to one event per player per matchday.** They fire off the same goal, and in a league's *first* season the two totals are identical, so every career milestone restated a season one (measured: 747 of 747). Higher tier wins; ties go to career.
  - **`compOf` is per-season (`seasonHistory[].compsByTid`), not a live team lookup.** Promotion and relegation move clubs, the user's included, so a live read re-files every past season under whichever division everyone is in now. The season in progress has no snapshot and uses live teams. An unresolved competition degrades to world-tier only — nobody's league, never everybody's.
  - Dashboard's headline panel runs through the same builder and is where this is most visible (it shows the last 5 items, which were previously almost always foreign).
  - **End-of-season awards are a third feed source, DERIVED rather than persisted (`core/awardNews.ts`, 2026-08-28).** Every honour is already on `SeasonHistoryEntry` (`awards` per competition, `world` for the worldwide ones), which is exactly the test `newsEvents.ts` states for what must be written at sim time — an accomplishment is persisted only because it *can't* be derived. These can, so they cost nothing in save size, can't drift from the Awards page, and **an existing save shows its whole back catalogue on load** rather than only seasons played after the change. `seasonAwardNews(entry)` flattens them; `awardNewsScope` tiers them (worldwide honours are world, a competition's own are league, and so is a Ballon d'Or placing behind the winner — sixteen leagues' runners-up would otherwise all reach every reader). Measured: **27-28 rows a season** (your league's 13 + the world's 14), ~3% of the feed.
    - **A winner's club comes from `awardWinners`, not from a live lookup**, with the Ballon d'Or's own `tid` preferred since the entry carries it. Neither is guaranteed on an old save (the snapshot is backfilled only as far as the pool and archive still reached), so `tid` is optional — which is why a **domestic** honour files by its `compId`, taken from the record's own key, instead of by the winner's club. It lands in the right league even when the save can no longer say where he played.
  - **Trophies are a fourth source, also derived (`core/trophyNews.ts`, 2026-08-29).** `trophyNewsBySeason` buckets the Continental Cup, the Continental Shield, the World Cup and every confederation cup by the season each was won in, off `CupState.championTid` and `IntlTournamentSummary.champion`. Same argument as the honours: the champion is already recorded, so a news event for one would duplicate the save's own record and could drift from it. **Takes no tier test** — each is one row for the whole world, and who won the Continental Cup is news wherever you play.
    - **Reads the LIVE cup as well as the archive.** A cup only moves into `cupHistory` at the offseason rollover, so reading the archive alone would hold the club season's biggest result back until the user clicked Advance. They can't double up: the same step that archives the old cup rebuilds the live one for the new season.
    - **An international tournament files under the club season it FOLLOWED**, since it is played in the offseason after it and stamped with it — the convention `worldAwards.ts` already uses for scoring the same campaign. So a World Cup sits beside the honours of the season before it, not the one after.
    - Ordered before the honours at the same season-end sentinel (`RANK` in `newsFeedTimeline.ts`): who won what, then who was best. On the Dashboard they need no club filter (a handful of rows against the honours' 27) and appear the moment they are won, because unlike an award a trophy *does* coexist with its own season.
    - **A nation is not a club**, so an international winner renders through `NationName` rather than `teamCell`, which needs a tid. Use `NationName` (or `Flag tip={false}`) wherever a country name sits beside its flag, or the tooltip repeats the name and the cell reads "England England".
    - **A season's awards never coexist with `league.season` being that season**, because the offseason that writes the history entry is the same step that moves the clock on. So the Dashboard panel — which is current-season — would show awards *never* if handed its own. It takes the **previous** season's instead, filtered to the user's own club and gated on `league.played.length === 0`, so they ride along until the new season kicks off and then drop out on their own. Filtering to his club is load-bearing: the full slate is 27 honours against a panel of 8.

**Finance**
- `budget` is a **cumulative running balance**, never reset to base — light spending compounds. Bounded by `budgetCap(tier, hype)` (`core/finance/budget.ts`): hype-scaled ceiling between `MAX_BUDGET_FLOOR` and `MAX_BUDGET`, ×`financeScale` per country/tier. Banking past the cap destroys the excess (never causes a deficit).
- Wages: cubic in ovr (`seasonSalaryForOvr`, `core/contracts.ts`), charged **up front** at season start (`chargeSeasonStart`); season-end `settleSeasonEnd` handles prize + hype − scouting only. Mid-season acquisitions charge full season salary at acquisition.
- **Value scale retuned to the game's own economy (2026-08-08).** `VALUATION_OVR_COEFF` 56_000→**32_000** and `VALUATION_ELITE_COEFF/EXPONENT` 11_000_000/2.5→**1_200_000/2.0**. The old curve was pinned to real Premier League *fees* while `BASE_SEASON_BUDGET` is $88M (a real club turns over several times that), so a "realistic" $120M fee cost ~1.4 seasons of a club's entire income vs ~0.2 for the real deal it copied — every transfer was proportionally ~8x the real market. Worse, the elite term **saturated**: at ovr 80 it alone was $352M, so *every* player ≥80 priced identically at the `MAX_TRANSFER_VALUE` clamp and (via `AI_MARKET_FEE_FLOOR_FRACTION` 0.5) every elite deal opened at $175M. Measured: **42 fees ≥$100M per season**, whose median subject was ovr 75 / age 22. New base+elite curve: 65≈$21M, 70≈$32M, 75≈$48M, 78≈$59M, 80≈$86M, 83≈$134M, 85≈$186M, 87≈$241M — a real gradient, clamp reserved for the freak. Also `AI_MARKET_AVAILABILITY` 1.05→**0.95** (above 1.0 a club shopped players it rated *above* market, the opposite of "surplus") — **that tightening was reverted by deleting the constant outright on 2026-08-11; it inverted the strength ladder. See the AI↔AI market entry above and `docs/transfer-mobility.md`.** **Gate for any future change here: `scripts/marketRealismAudit.ts`** (star churn, downward moves, ≥$100M count, club solvency, FA-pool size). Beware the coupled constants — lowering values makes renewals *less* likely (value vs `salary × AI_RENEWAL_MARGIN`) and shrinks loan/`AI_MARKET_MIN_VALUE` eligibility.
- **Capped values + "protected star" not-for-sale gate** — the load-bearing **difficulty lever** (money buys a good squad, not a great one). Two parts: (1) an accelerating elite premium above `VALUATION_ELITE_THRESHOLD` (76 ≈ the top of a league) in `trueTransferValue` makes the real difference-makers expensive, but every value/asking price is **clamped at `MAX_TRANSFER_VALUE` ($350M)** so quoted fees stay believable — no more billion-dollar tags. (2) The genuine best players are removed from the market entirely rather than priced into the stratosphere: `core/transfers/protectedStars.ts` `isProtectedStar`/`protectedStarPids` withhold any AI player who was *best-in-world last season* (`ovr ≥ PROTECTED_STAR_OVR` 80, **or** won POTY/Golden Boot/a Team-of-the-Season place) **and** whose club finished **top-`PROTECTED_STAR_TOP_FINISH` (4)** of a **tier-1** league last season. Wired into `makeTransferOffer`/`acceptCounterOffer` (user buys), `recommendedTransfers` (hidden from targets), and `runAITransferMarket` (AI↔AI, via a `protectedPids` set passed by the caller). The gate is **data-driven off a completed-season record**, not the live league, because the offseason summer market runs *before* the `seasonHistory` entry is appended — that caller (`offseason.ts`) builds the record from the freshly-computed `standings`/`awards`/`compsByTid`; regular-season/winter callers use `lastCompletedSeason(league)`. The **user's own club is never gated** (you always control your sales). Note a global player-quality nerf can *not* substitute for gating the market, because match composites z-normalize league-wide quality away (proven — it left title-win rates unchanged).
- **Finance page** (`/finance`) — pure display of budget/hype, scouting slider, settlement breakdown, wage bill, transfer history, league-wide table.
  - **Organised around WHEN money moves, not what it totals (2026-09-03, user ask: "make it much more transparent... this isn't just about what is on the page, but also the design").** The finance model is almost entirely a calendar — wages charged in full at season *start*, league prize and hype revenue at season *end*, cup prize the day the tie is played, add-ons in the offseason — and a page of equal-weight cards stated none of it. Two bespoke surfaces now carry that: a dense `.fin-strip` (balance / wage bill / projected next-season start / hype, each with a proportion meter) and `.fin-year`, a four-stage timeline marking the stage the save is in. The old "Projected Offseason Cash Flow" table is gone into the timeline, which fixes its worst property: it ran three season-*end* rows and two next-season-*start* rows as one flat list, so the arithmetic spanned two seasons with nothing saying so. The Cup Prize Money card folded into the during-the-season stage for the same reason. Deliberately dense rather than airy — DESIGN.md rules out hero-metric cards and names the box score's scoreboard as the house idiom for this.
  - **The projection did not apply `clampBudget`, and that is a real bug rather than a presentation one.** It quoted `budget + net`, a raw sum, where the offseason runs `settleSeasonEnd` then `chargeSeasonStart` and **both clamp at `budgetCap`**. A club near its ceiling was therefore shown money that would be destroyed on arrival. It now runs the two real functions in the real order. Two consequences worth keeping: the projection is still an estimate (hype moves *between* the two steps, so the second clamp's true ceiling is unknowable in advance), and `financeRender.test.tsx` pins it by putting a club **on** its cap and reading the projected figure back out of the strip — verified to fail against the old `budget + net` form, so it catches a wrong number rather than missing copy.
  - **The savings ceiling is surfaced for the first time, and it is the one mechanic here that destroys money silently.** `clampBudget` caps every credit, so anything banked above `budgetCap` is never paid at all; nothing on any screen said so. The balance tile shows how full the ceiling is, and a warning quotes what a projection would lose to it before it happens.
  - **Three honesty rules the page has to keep.** (a) The in-season line is **transfer FEES**, never "spent" — a mid-season signing also charges his season wages up front (`executeTransfer`) and nothing records that, so a reconciliation to the balance is not derivable and must not be implied; the running line is "Net so far this season" because the summer window is stamped with the season it opens and the figure is routinely negative. (b) A **rank is not quoted before the division has played anything** — `computeStandings` on an empty fixture list returns array order, so "1st of 20" is a fabrication; `table.started` gates it. (c) A sell-on with no agreed fee renders **"—", not "$0"** — those are different claims, and $0 says the clause is worthless.
- **Cup prize money is shown on the Finance page and is DERIVED, not stored (`core/finance/prizeIncome.ts`, 2026-09-02, user ask).** A cup prize is a pure function of results the save already keeps, so `seasonPrizeIncome` replays them for one club and reports what it banked, broken down by competition and round. Same call the trophy and honours news make, and it buys the same two things: **no persisted field and no `migrate.ts` change**, and it works **retroactively** — an existing save shows its current season's cup earnings the moment the page opens, where a new counter would have started from zero. The card sits between the settlement projection and the wage bill and is deliberately about *banked* money: cup prizes are credited the day the tie is played, unlike league prize money, which the card above it projects to season end.
  - **The cost is a second copy of the prize rules, and two things hold it to the sim.** Every *amount* comes from the sim's own helpers (`cupFormat().prizes`, `koPrizeByRound`, `domesticPrizeForRound`, `domesticCupScaleFor`) rather than being re-read from constants, so no retune of a prize can pass it by. The *rules* — which events pay at all — are pinned by `test/core/prizeIncome.test.ts`, which plays a whole English domestic cup through `playDomesticRound`, accumulates the `prizes` maps the sim actually returns, and asserts the derivation matches **per club and in aggregate**. Without that test the failure is silent and reaches a player as "my budget went up by more than the page says".
  - **Two edges worth knowing.** Participation is credited when league-phase round 0 is *played*, not at the draw, so a club that has qualified and not yet kicked off has banked nothing and the card must say so (pinned). And the domestic side must apply `domesticCupScaleFor`, not `financeScaleFor` — country-scaled, tier-flat — or a lower-division club's card under-reports the cheque it actually received; the test passes the real scale rather than a flat 1 for exactly that reason.

**Club debt (`core/finance/debt.ts`, `LeagueStore.debtSanctions`, user's club only, 2026-09-08)**
- **The state already existed and nothing was reading it.** `chargeSeasonStart` subtracts the wage bill with no floor and `clampBudget` is a `Math.min`, so a negative balance has always passed straight through and persisted — it is exactly what `weakLeaguesAudit` reports when it says an AI club is at −£5.1M. What was missing was the ability to *choose* it, any consequence, and any way to see it. So this adds no balance field: **debt is simply `budget < 0`**.
- **Three rungs, in the order real football applies them.** An **overdraft** (`OVERDRAFT_LIMIT_FRACTION` 0.75 of the club's own scaled base income, ~$66M at a big-four top flight) you may deliberately spend into; a **registration embargo** if you end a season past `DEBT_EMBARGO_THRESHOLD` (0.4) of it; a **points deduction** on next season's table if you end it past `DEBT_DEDUCTION_THRESHOLD` (0.75), 6 points rising 3 a season in breach to a cap of 12. `DEBT_INTEREST_RATE` (0.1) is charged at season start on a negative balance and compounds by construction (it deepens the balance the next charge is measured against). One clear season resets the streak, since a season with no record reads a streak of 0.
- **User-club-only, and that containment is the entire safety argument — it is why this shipped with NO dynasty audit.** Nothing here reaches AI↔AI trading, the country ladder or the anti-inflation equilibrium; AI clubs keep going quietly negative exactly as they do today. Same property every difficulty lever has. `debt.test.ts` pins it directly by bankrupting all 625 AI clubs and asserting zero sanctions. **Widening any of it world-wide means `scripts/weakLeaguesAudit.ts` on both sides first**, and note `main` is currently red on that gate for unrelated reasons.
- **The penalty is SPORTING, never a board-confidence hit, and that is load-bearing rather than flavour.** `deriveExpectations` is built so no sequence of transfer decisions can move the bar the board judges you against — two money terms were built and removed for exactly that reason, because balance-plus-wage-bill still lets a *teardown* lower it. A deduction touches none of that machinery: it makes you genuinely finish lower and the board judges the lower finish through the channel it already had. Wiring debt into expectations instead reopens the teardown exploit.
- **`computeStandings` takes an optional `deductions` map** and `StandingsRow` gained an optional `deducted`. Omitting it leaves every row untouched, so the 24 existing call sites are byte-identical. Points come off **after** every match is counted and **before** the sort, or a docked club sits where its undocked total put it. Threaded into every table a human reads (Standings, both Dashboards, Finance, the live viewer, `clubSeason`) and every table that *decides* something (`offseason`'s final tables → promotion, prize money, European places; `seasonQualification`; `manager`'s review; the promotion-playoff seeding in `simThrough`).
  - **Deliberately NOT `ai/clubContext.ts`**, which is the one table left alone. That one is a *form* proxy feeding the AI's own strength model, and a financial sanction has not made the club's football worse — docking it would quietly make a fined club's players cheaper and the club less attractive to join. It would also mean widening `LeagueSnapshot`, which is kept minimal on purpose. The call site carries the note so nobody "fixes" it.
  - **The row has to carry `deducted`** or the points column stops reconciling with its own W/D/L, which reads as a bug rather than a penalty. Standings renders it as a red `(-6)`.
- **Assessed at offseason step 3.55, off the balance AFTER `settleSeasonEnd`.** That is the club's year-end position, so prize money and hype revenue can rescue a marginal club (pinned) and **selling in July cannot undo last season's breach** — which is the real dynamic and the reason the Dashboard and Finance page warn *during* the season, keyed off `UserDebtView.projected`. A warning that only arrives with the penalty gives the player no chance to trade out of it.
- **The overdraft bounds VOLUNTARY spending only, and the asymmetry is the point.** Wages are charged whatever the balance says, so a club can still be carried past its limit involuntarily — which is precisely the "you owe more than you thought" state the sanctions price. **That case is already reachable and was doing nothing:** `offseason.ts` settles transfer-clause bonuses with a bare `t.budget + d`, no floor and no check, so a big appearance bonus could push the user into the red and *nothing whatsoever happened*. A hard floor on the balance would instead make the debt silently vanish, which teaches the player nothing.
- **`SpendPolicy` is built by the caller and passed in, so the core paths keep their narrow signatures**, and **an absent policy means the old cash-in-hand rule** — which is what keeps every path this is not wired into behaving exactly as before. Above all `ensureUserRosterSafety`, which **must never be blocked**: a club that cannot field eleven crashes the engine (`pickInterceptor` dereferences an undefined tackler) rather than degrading. Gated: `makeTransferOffer` and `signFreeAgent` take both overdraft and embargo; `signToAcademy`/`promoteFromAcademy`/`signTrialist` take the overdraft and **ignore the embargo**, because a sanctioned club can still run its own academy and that is the cheapest route out. Loans need no gate — there is no user loan-in path.
- **A sanction belongs to the CLUB, not the manager, so `switchClub` deliberately does not clear it** — leave a club you have run into a deduction and it serves that deduction after you have gone, which is both what real football does and the one way this can touch a club the user no longer manages. Bounded and one-directional: at most one club, only in a season following a user breach, and it can only ever cost that club points. It cannot move money or ratings, so it stays far below the noise the ladder audit is measuring. Worth knowing before reading the containment claim above as absolute.
- **News is DERIVED (`core/debtNews.ts`), never a stored `NewsEvent`** — same argument as `trophyNews`/`awardNews`/`promotionNews`: the sanction is already on `debtSanctions`, so an event row would be a second copy that could drift, and a save already sanctioned reports it the moment this ships. **No `DebtNews` wrapper type**, deliberately: the other three each derive something the raw record lacks (a normalised cup `kind`, a finishing position, a scoreline) while a sanction already carries every field the headline needs. Bucketed by the season it *applies to*, and sorted at `PRESEASON_ORDER` **ahead of the summer transfers** (`RANK` puts `debt` at 0) because an embargo governs the window about to open — the rollover has already moved `league.season` on, so the summer window it forbids is that season's. Shown for the user's club **or anyone in his competition**: the second half is not redundant, because a sanction belongs to the club and one he left still serves it. At most one row a season, so no volume tiering is needed. The Dashboard panel reads the *end* of the timeline, so a preseason item correctly ages out there while the Finances card's standing warning does not.
- **Zero rng draws**, so scorelines are bit-identical and no dynasty baseline moves. `ui/userDebt.ts` is the single derivation shared by the actions, the Finance panel and the Dashboard warning, so the page quoting your headroom and the button refusing the signing cannot disagree.
- **The Finance page carries a permanent "Borrowing and debt" section stating the rules, shown whether or not the club is in any trouble.** The three alerts above it only fire once a penalty is coming, and a rule the player can only read after it has bitten him is one he could never have planned around. It reuses the money year's `fin-year` timeline because the rules genuinely are a ladder of rungs, and the marker follows the balance. **Every figure is derived** — thresholds from `userDebtView`, the deduction and the interest rate straight from the constants — so a retune cannot leave the copy quoting a line that has moved; `financeRender.test.tsx` pins the marker landing on the right rung at three depths. It also fixed a sign inconsistency this section made obvious: a `plain` money line went through `currency.format`, whose hyphen sat in the same column as `Amount`'s typographic minus, so `balanceText` now renders every balance and the money year's running totals came along with it.
- **A jumped or spectated save is skipped for free**: no club holds `AUTOPILOT_TID` or `SPECTATOR_TID`, so the roster lookup returns undefined and no sentinel check is needed — just as well, since importing either into `offseason.ts` closes a cycle (`autopilot.ts` imports it).
- Optional field, `migrate.ts` backfills `[]`; absent is exact rather than a guess, because nothing was assessing those seasons. Copy on the Finance page, the Dashboard and the Manual's Difficulty section previously said "you can't sign anyone" and "there's no interest to pay" and had to be rewritten — grep for stale claims when a mechanic like this lands.

**Difficulty** (`DIFFICULTIES` in `constants.ts`, `LeagueStore.difficulty`, 2026-08-17)
- Four levels (easy/normal/hard/brutal) chosen on `/new-league`, **fixed for the save's lifetime**, migrated to `"normal"` for old saves. `normal`'s profile is every lever's identity value or the shipped constant, so an existing dynasty is provably unchanged (pinned by `difficulty.test.ts`).
- **Every lever is user-club-only, and that is a hard constraint rather than a convenience.** AI↔AI trading, the country strength ladder and the anti-inflation equilibrium are one tuned system. The specific trap: widening the protected-star gate *world-wide* on the hard levels is the obvious implementation and would cut AI↔AI deal volume, which lands straight on the documented tripwire (the weak leagues run on transfer receipts; the last change that reduced deal flow put 2 of 4 seeds into deficit with every strength check green). Because nothing reaches the AI, **no difficulty change needs a dynasty audit** — that property is worth preserving.
- Five levers and their seams: **money** — `financeScaleFor` (`finance/budget.ts`) is the *one* seam, wrapping `financeScale` and multiplying for `userTid` only; it must scale income **and** the savings ceiling, since `clampBudget` would otherwise destroy an easy level's bonus. **Academy** — an intake-time addend beside `academyFormModifiers` in `offseason.ts`, **never** written into `StoredTeam.academyBase` (promotion convergence would drag it back toward the competition centre and roster-import realignment would permute it onto another club). **Buy price** — `reservationPrice`'s new `priceScale`, whose single call site is `makeTransferOffer`, plus `scoutedValue` on the two buy-side display surfaces; both still clamp at the unscaled `MAX_TRANSFER_VALUE` so no level quotes a fantasy fee. **Market access** — `ProtectedStarBar` on `isProtectedStar`/`protectedStarPids`, defaulting to the shipped constants; only the four user-facing call sites pass `userProtectedStarBar(difficulty)`. **Fog** — a `fogScale` on `potentialFog` (half-width *and* clearing time), purely informational, no rng draw.
- **Opening budget scales the post-wage SURPLUS, every later season scales income.** Deliberately asymmetric: scaling the base allocation at creation starts a Brutal save at an expensive club already in the red before the user has made a decision, whereas a post-wage figure is positive by construction. From season 2 on `chargeSeasonStart` takes the scale normally and going broke becomes a consequence of how the club is run.
- **Going broke is reachable by choice, not by construction, and that's the calibration.** Measured (`scripts/difficultyProbe.ts` + the wage curve): a mid-table generated squad costs ~£18M/season against £88M base income, so nobody starts in trouble; but a 30-man squad averaging ovr 75 costs **£88.6M**, i.e. break-even on normal and a **~£18M/season deficit on hard** (£70.4M income), ~£36M on brutal. Beware measuring this in a headless audit — the unmanaged user club rots to academy stipends and understates its wage bill ~5×, which made the squeeze look far too weak on the first pass.
- Bankruptcy is survivable and already half-implemented by existing code: `makeTransferOffer` gates on budget so you simply can't buy, and `clampScoutingSpend`'s `Math.max(budget, 0)` switches scouting off, which widens the fog. Both are surfaced (Finance in-the-red alert, Dashboard warning); there is still **no debt mechanic** and open decision #2 is unchanged.
- **Difficulty diverges the rng stream** (it changes what the user can afford), so two levels on one seed are different worlds and cross-world counts are not comparable. `difficultyProbe.ts` therefore applies all four bars to a *single* simmed world; a probe that sims each level separately will report a moving AI-side count and look like a leak that isn't one.
- Copy gotcha: on hard levels a player can be unbuyable by the user while still moving between AI clubs, so the not-for-sale reason switches to **"Not available to you"** rather than claiming his club won't sell (`protectedStarReason` in `recommendations.ts`).

**Development model — random growth vs steady careers (`ProgressionModel`, `LeagueStore.progressionModel`, 2026-09-08, user ask)**
- Two development models per save. `"random"` is the shipped one and the default. `"steady"` turns the per-season dice down so a career reads as one arc — grow through the early twenties, hold through the peak, decline from thirty at an accelerating rate — which is the request verbatim ("rng has been ceiling some irl careers, and it makes the league best players fake players too quickly"). Chosen on New League, and **changeable mid-save in God Mode → Development**, unlike `difficulty` and `rollingCoefficients` beside it.
- **The whole of `"steady"` is: the shipped model with the form roll turned down and the age curve redrawn.** `PROGRESSION_PROFILES` bundles every model-dependent term into one record and **restates the shipped constants by reference, never by value**, so a retune of `PROGRESSION_FORM_SD_YOUNG` reaches both models and the default is provably the game as it was (`progressionModel.test.ts` pins it field by field).
- **The model scales draws, never the draw COUNT** — both `gaussian(rng)` calls happen whatever the profile says, and `gaussian` is exactly two `rng()` draws with no rejection loop. That single property is what makes the setting safe to flip mid-save, what lets `"random"` be byte-identical, and what keeps a steady world's *ratings* identical to a random one's at generation (only the potential forecast moves — a test asserts exactly that).
- **Two hand-reasoned constants were built, measured and DELETED, and both are the conclusion anyone reasoning from first principles reaches.** Record them before rebuilding either. **(a) "With the form roll gone, careers will all look the same, so widen the per-player bias."** No: the form roll is zero-mean and re-rolled every season, so it very largely cancels over a career and contributes far less to how careers *end up* than to how they *move*. At the shipped bias of 3 the spread of plateau ratings is already **92%** of random's; widening it to 5 blew the elite tier open instead (peak p90 **80 → 90**, cohort peaking 85+ **0.9% → 19.5%**). **(b) "`growthDamping` was sized to suppress lucky jumps, so with no luck left it will wall the league off below 80."** Measured, the opposite: elite is *more* reachable under steady development at the shipped 80/0.02 (**80+ in 3.9% of plateau player-seasons against random's 2.7%**), because a consistent bias pushes through a damping band better than a coin-flipping roll that spends half its seasons undoing the other half. Relaxing damping *and* widening bias was the single worst combination swept.
- **`peak ovr` is the wrong objective and cost an hour of tuning the wrong thing.** A peak is a *maximum over a trajectory*, so noise inflates it structurally — random's peaks read **65.6 against the 58.5 its players actually carry**. Matching steady's peaks to random's would not make the worlds equivalent, it would make steady players genuinely better. The tell was a 70+ share stuck ~14 points below random under *every* combination of three levers: **a gap no lever moves is usually a gap in the question.** The sweep now targets mean ovr over ages 24-32 and the share of player-seasons at each bar — what a league actually fields.
- **The plateau cannot be drawn directly, and that is a property of the engine rather than of the curve.** A rating group reads the curve at a *shifted* age (`PHYSICAL_AGE_SHIFT` +3, `SKILL_AGE_SHIFT` −1.5), so at any real age the two groups sample points **4.5 years apart** and no flat window holds both still across 24-30. An ovr plateau is physicals easing off cancelling against skills still inching up — so `STEADY_AGE_CURVE` is *widened* around the peak, not squared off.
- **The decline tail is the inflation guard and it was swept, not drawn.** Widening the plateau inherently holds ratings up, so the tail has to give it back or a steady league's mean ratchets. First hand-drawn tail: plateau ovr **+1.5** over random and survival-weighted lifetime drift **−2.81 → −0.86**, i.e. most of the way from "slightly deflationary" to "flat". Pulling the mid-30s down puts both back (**+1.3 / −3.87**, a shade *more* deflationary than shipped, which is the safe side to miss on); a third steeper tail overshoots to **−7.27**. `scripts/steadySweep.ts` keeps all three so the choice stays reproducible.
- **`model` is a trailing optional defaulting to `"random"`, so a forgotten call site degrades to the shipped model rather than to nonsense** — but there are exactly three paths that must pass it (world generation, youth intake, roster import, all via `generatePlayer`; plus the offseason's progression pass) and `progressionModel.test.ts` pins each. The one that matters most is **youth intake**: an academy kid's listed potential is what the scholarship and professional cuts rank him on (`core/academyPipeline.ts`), so a forecast from the wrong model would decide who the academy keeps.
- Tools: `scripts/progressionModelProbe.ts` (arc / unpredictability / spread / ceiling / inflation / forecast, seconds) and `scripts/steadySweep.ts` (the constant sweep). `weakLeaguesAudit.ts` takes `MODEL=steady`, defaulting to `"random"` so every number recorded against it elsewhere in this file still measures what it measured. **The two models are not comparable run for run** — different worlds from the first offseason — so read each against its own gates.
- **Measuring unpredictability needs care: raw |ovr change| conflates movement with randomness.** A 17-year-old gaining 5 is the age curve working and a 37-year-old losing 5 is the decline working; both are meant to be large and both are perfectly predictable. The probe reports a player's change **around his own average** at that life stage, which is the part that is genuinely a surprise. Measured that way (800 careers, 2 seeds): growth years **sd 4.63 → 1.96** and shock seasons (5+ from his own norm) **28.4% → 2.2%**; peak years **2.93 → 1.26** and **9.4% → 0.2%**; decline years unchanged (2.04 → 2.11), which is correct — a decline is deterministic under both models.
- **"The variance moved rather than being deleted" is verified, not asserted: peak-ovr sd is 13.8 random against 13.9 steady**, and p90 80 against 79. Careers still differ from each other by the same amount; only *when* the difference is settled has changed.
- **`POTENTIAL_SIM_PERCENTILE` (0.75) stops meaning what it says under `"steady"`, and that is a semantic change rather than a bug.** The estimate is the 75th percentile of 16 simulated peaks, which only reads as "most players fall short" while the trials genuinely disagree; under steady they nearly agree, so the percentile collapses onto the expected peak. Measured at rotation minutes, players who *beat* their listed potential: random **36%**, steady **59.5%**, against a design intent of ~25%. So POT stops being an optimistic ceiling and becomes a forecast of where a player ends up — which is the feature working rather than failing, and is why scouting is worth more on a steady save. The Manual says so explicitly; **do not "fix" it by raising the percentile for steady** without deciding that an optimistic ceiling is what that save wants.
- **EVERY absolute potential threshold in the game slides out from under a steady save at once, and this is the trap the model most easily hides (`potentialBar`, `STEADY_POTENTIAL_BAR_OFFSET` −11).** An honest forecast sits below an optimistic one, so listed potentials fall across the board (mean 67.8 → 60.5; a real youth intake's median **53 → 42**) — and every gate that reads one is a fixed number. Measured before the fix, the share of an intake clearing `AI_PROSPECT_MIN_POT` fell **18.7% → 7.8%** and `RETIREMENT_PROSPECT_POT_THRESHOLD` **24.7% → 11.7%** (both measured pre-lift, when the bars read 70 and 65), i.e. AI clubs stopped protecting the wonderkids `AI_PROSPECT_SLOTS` exists to protect and young free agents washed out of the pool — half-reopening #318 and the retirement rework, silently, via a setting that mentions neither. **The invariant is the SHARE, not the number**: both bars are defined by what they are *for* ("genuine wonderkids, not every teenager"), which is a statement about where a player sits in his own intake — the fifth instance in this file of *comparable within a population, not across populations*. The offset is measured rather than chosen — on the shipped scale **81 → 70 and 76 → 65**, the same −11 on both — and re-derives itself in `progressionModelProbe.ts` section G; it is **0 for `"random"`, so every shipped bar is untouched**. Any *new* potential gate must go through `potentialBar`.
  - **It is NOT a constant in rating points, and `OVR_SCALE_SHIFT` (#341/#345) proved it — re-derive after a SCALE change, not just after a `STEADY_*` one.** The gap between the two forecasts comes from the noise, which is measured in rating points and did not move when every rating was lifted +11, so the offset should have been untouched; it was −12 before the lift and −11 after. The missing point is the **`RATING_MAX` ceiling**: random's potential is the 75th percentile of sixteen noisy futures, so it is the one with an optimistic upper tail, and lifting the scale clips that tail against 99 more often than it clips steady's near-deterministic one. Measured, median listed potential moved **+10 for random against +11 for steady** — the two distributions did not translate by the same amount. The re-derivation reads a percentile off an integer distribution, so it wobbles a point with sample size; take the value both bars agree on.
  - **Everything else about the model was immune to the lift, and that is the useful half of the lesson.** The curve and the form sd are *deltas*, and a uniform additive shift leaves a delta alone — re-measured across the merge, unpredictability in the growth years reads 4.63 → 1.96 before and **4.61 → 1.99** after, shock seasons 28.4% → 2.2% and **28.2% → 2.2%**, career-spread sd 13.8/13.9 and **13.3/13.5**. So when a scale change lands, the only thing to re-derive here is the one constant that sits on an absolute bar.
- **Two further knock-ons are accepted rather than fixed, both consequences of an honest forecast.** **(a) `peakOvr` falls** (65.2 → 62.4 mean) because a peak is a max over a trajectory and there is less noise to spike it — so fewer retirees clear `RETIREE_ARCHIVE_MIN_PEAK_OVR` (70) and a steady save's archive leans harder on the 200-appearance route. **(b) Keepers are the one position that drifts UP** (survival-weighted lifetime delta +0.15 → +3.05, against every other position going more negative and the pool as a whole −3.67 → −4.54): `GK_AGE_SHIFT` has them read the curve younger still, so they sit longest in the widened plateau. Watch that one first if a steady dynasty ever looks keeper-heavy.
- **No dynasty audit is needed for the DEFAULT, and that is provable rather than asserted**: draw counts are identical, both profiles restate the shipped constants by reference, `potentialBar`'s offset is 0 for `"random"`, and the `touchStats` scoreline hash plus all 28 validation gates and 74 existing progression/positionChange/migrate tests pass unchanged. **`"steady"` is a different matter and DOES need one** — it is a different progression model, and progression is what the country ladder and weak-league solvency both rest on. Run `MODEL=steady SEASONS=20 SEEDS=1,2,3,4 npx tsx scripts/weakLeaguesAudit.ts`, and note the two models are **not comparable run for run** (different worlds from the first offseason), so read steady against the script's own gates rather than against a random-model run.
- **Audited on the merged tree, `MODEL=steady SEASONS=20 SEEDS=1,2,3,4`: every seed `ladder OK`, every seed `SOLVENT`, `RESULT: all checks passed`.** Pooled rung means BIG4→France **+4.97**, Netherlands→Portugal +1.82, Portugal→Belgium +1.10, Belgium→Turkey +0.71, with block spreads BIG4→Serbia **+9.04** (gate ≥4) and France→Serbia **+4.07** (gate ≥1). Turkey→Greece −0.95 is the only negative and sits just inside the ±1 tolerance, in the converged bottom block the script deliberately does not gate per-seed. **Solvency is the column this feature could plausibly have broken and it is clean on all four: 0 of 625 clubs in deficit at 0 of 42 sampled points**, minimum budget landing at season 1-3 — the documented pre-existing knife-edge, not a dynasty-long ratchet. Read that against `origin/main` itself having failed the same gate as recently as #309.
  - **Run split across both Macs, so the eight rung values were pooled BY HAND.** `weakLeaguesAudit` aggregates across seeds, so `auditCluster.sh` refuses to split it without `--per-seed-only`, and under that flag each half's own cross-seed mean is over its own subset and is **not** the number to read. Seeds 1-3 ran locally in one invocation (that half's 3-seed mean is legitimate); seed 4 ran on the second machine.
  - **A `MODEL=random` CONTROL on the identical tree and seeds is what made the result readable, and without it I would have reported a regression that does not exist.** France→Serbia read +3.45/+2.99 on the merged tree against +6.17/+5.38 on the same seeds before the merge, which looks like a halving. The control reads **+3.19 and +7.42** — a four-point swing across two seeds *with the default model*. That rung simply is not resolvable at two seeds on this codebase, the steady values sit inside the control's own range, and the pooled four-seed figure (+4.07) lands near the pre-merge reading anyway. **Nth instance of the standing lesson: compare against a control on the same tree, never against your own earlier numbers on a different one.**
  - **`auditCluster.sh` runs the remote half INLINE over ssh, so a dropped link kills it mid-run** — measured here, exit 255 and `Timeout, server ... not responding` after one generation line, with nothing usable. Launch a long remote audit with `nohup` **on the remote side** and poll its log instead; the link can then drop harmlessly. Unfixed in the script.
